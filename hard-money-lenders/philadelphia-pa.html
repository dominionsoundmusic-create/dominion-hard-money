Hard Money Lenders in Philadelphia: Strong Margins, Tighter Rules

Philadelphia rowhouse flips still carry some of the widest margins in the country, minus a 4.578 percent transfer tax.

Send us your Philadelphia dealPennsylvania investor guide

Wide margins on paper

Philadelphia is one of the most profitable flipping markets in the country on paper. ATTOM's report on the second quarter of 2026 put the typical gross flipping margin in the Philadelphia metro at 62.8 percent, fifth among large metros and nearly three times the national figure of 21.5 percent. Rowhouses bought cheaply, renovated and sold to a steady stream of buyers can still produce real profit here.

Gross margin is the gap between the purchase price and the resale price, before renovation, points, interest, insurance, taxes and commissions. In Philadelphia two local costs take a larger share of that gap than they would almost anywhere else: the transfer tax and the paperwork that comes with renting.

The financing side works on the same terms as elsewhere. Take a rowhouse bought for $150,000 with $60,000 of work and an after-repair value of $300,000. Seventy percent of $300,000 is $210,000, which would cover the whole cost under our lending partners' standard limits of 100 percent of cost and 70 percent of after-repair value. Leverage still depends on your documented experience and on the property, so confirm the maximum loan for your Philadelphia address with the lender before you go under contract.

The transfer tax is 4.578 percent

The city's realty transfer tax rose on July 1, 2025 to 3.578 percent for the city plus 1 percent for Pennsylvania: 4.578 percent of the price in total, one of the highest rates in the country. By custom it is split between buyer and seller, though the city can collect all of it from either side.

On that same flip, your buyer's half at purchase is about $3,430 and your seller's half at resale is about $6,870: roughly $10,300 in transfer tax on one project. Across the city line in Delaware, Montgomery, Bucks or Chester County, the combined rate is usually far lower, so a suburban rowhouse or twin with a thinner spread can net the same money. Check the exact rate for the municipality and school district.

Renting in Philadelphia takes paperwork

Philadelphia regulates rentals more closely than most cities. Anyone renting a housing unit needs a Housing Rental License. Before a new tenant moves in, the landlord must give them a Certificate of Rental Suitability issued by Licenses and Inspections no more than 60 days before the lease starts. For properties built before 1978, which describes much of the city, the landlord also needs a lead-safe or lead-free certificate from a state-certified lead inspector or certified dust-wipe technician, based on an inspection no more than four years old, submitted to the Department of Public Health. Missing paperwork can keep a landlord from collecting rent or evicting.

For an investor who plans to hold, build those steps into the renovation: schedule the lead dust-wipe test after the work is done and before the tenant signs, and apply for the license early. A rental with its license and certifications in place is also a cleaner file for a DSCR loan, which qualifies on the rent.

Property tax and the 2027 values

Philadelphia's real estate tax rate is 1.3998 percent of assessed value, a rate unchanged since 2016: 0.6159 percent for the city and 0.7839 percent for the School District. The Office of Property Assessment mailed new tax year 2027 values in summer 2026, effective January 1, 2027.

Look up the property's 2027 assessed value on the city's property site and multiply by 1.3998 percent. That, not the seller's old bill, is the figure for a rental budget and a DSCR payment. Homestead relief applies only to an owner-occupant, so an investor should not count on it.

Can I get a hard money loan to flip a Philadelphia rowhouse?

How much is the Philadelphia transfer tax?

Since July 1, 2025, 4.578 percent of the price: 3.578 percent for the city and 1 percent for Pennsylvania. It is customarily split between buyer and seller, so a flip pays about half going in and half coming out.

Are Philadelphia flip margins still good in 2026?

On gross numbers, yes. ATTOM put the typical gross flipping margin in the Philadelphia metro at 62.8 percent in the second quarter of 2026, fifth among large metros. Gross margin is before renovation, financing, taxes and selling costs, and the transfer tax takes a real share.

Do I need a rental license to rent out a house in Philadelphia?

Yes. You need a Housing Rental License, a Certificate of Rental Suitability for each new tenant, and for most properties built before 1978, a lead-safe or lead-free certificate based on an inspection no more than four years old.

What property tax will I pay on a Philadelphia rental?

The rate is 1.3998 percent of assessed value. Use the property's new tax year 2027 assessment from the city's property site, not the seller's old bill.

Is the transfer tax lower in the Philadelphia suburbs?

Usually, yes. Municipalities in Delaware, Montgomery, Bucks and Chester counties generally charge far less than the city's 3.578 percent local share. Check the exact rate for the municipality and school district before you offer.
