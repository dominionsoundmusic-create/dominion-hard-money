Hard Money Lenders in Maine: Where Financing Works and What It Costs

Most of Maine's financeable investment property sits within about an hour of Portland, and Maine's landlord law is specific.

Maine's investor map is mostly southern

Maine investors deal with a smaller map than most. This page shows where the deals cluster, what Maine's landlord law requires of a buy-and-hold investor, what a flip costs in transfer tax, and how the foreclosure rules shape a lender's view, then lays out our lending partners' terms.

Greater Portland is the core: Portland, South Portland, Westbrook, Scarborough and the towns around Casco Bay. Southern Maine sits close by: Biddeford and Saco, Sanford, and the York County towns toward the New Hampshire line. Lewiston and Auburn are roughly 40 minutes from Portland, Brunswick and Bath closer still, and Augusta about an hour.

Bangor is Maine's third-largest city but sits about two hours from Portland, and Down East, the western mountains and Aroostook County are more rural still. Private lenders commonly look harder at property where an appraiser has few recent comparable sales and a finished house has a thin pool of buyers. If your deal is north of Augusta, ask about the exact address before you spend money on inspections.

Maine's landlord rules, for buy-and-hold investors

Maine's landlord-tenant law is statewide and fairly specific. The points that shape a rental's numbers:

Security deposits are capped at two months' rent (14 M.R.S. 6032), must be held separately from the landlord's own funds, and must be returned within 30 days after a written lease ends, or 21 days after a tenancy at will, with an itemized statement.

Rent increases need at least 45 days' written notice, and 75 days if the increase is 10 percent or more, including several smaller increases within a year that add up to that (14 M.R.S. 6015). The requirement covers all rental housing and cannot be waived.

Late rent cannot draw a late fee until it is 15 days past due, and the fee is capped at 4 percent of a month's rent and must be disclosed in writing at the start of the tenancy (14 M.R.S. 6028).

Termination notices are short for cause: seven days when rent is seven or more days in arrears or for serious lease violations, and 30 days to end a tenancy at will.

Local rules can add more. Maine has no statewide rent control, but Portland has its own rent stabilization ordinance covering many units. If you are buying a rental inside Portland, read the city's current rules before you set rent projections, because they can cap how fast the rent can rise.

What Maine adds to a deal's costs

Transfer tax. Maine's real estate transfer tax is $2.20 per $500 of value, split equally between buyer and seller (36 M.R.S. 4641-A). On a $300,000 house each side pays about $660. A flip pays its half twice, buying and selling, so budget roughly $1,300 on a deal that size. Since November 1, 2025, a higher rate applies to transfers above $1 million, which matters on larger multifamily deals.

Property tax. Maine property tax is set town by town, and rates vary a great deal between neighboring towns. Maine's homestead exemption is only for residents' own primary homes, so a rental never gets it. Ask the town assessor for the current rate and the date of the last revaluation before you estimate holding costs.

Winter. A Maine renovation plan has to account for heating a vacant house, frozen pipes and a shorter exterior work season. A flip bought in November with exterior work planned should count on spring for that part of the job, and the loan term should allow for it.

Foreclosure goes through the courts. Maine forecloses judicially, and for mortgages signed since October 1, 1975 the owner has 90 days after the foreclosure judgment to redeem (14 M.R.S. 6322). Court time plus that window is part of why lenders pay close attention to the exit plan.

Our lending partners' terms in Maine

Fix and flip, rehab and bridge loans: pricing starts at 12.99 percent plus 2.99 points. Borrowers who have paid off two loans with the lending partner in good standing can qualify for loyalty pricing of 10.99 percent plus 1.99 points. Loans start at $50,000 with a 600 minimum credit score, and cover up to 100 percent of cost (purchase plus rehab) but no more than 70 percent of after-repair value, whichever binds first. Experience documented over the past three years sets how close you get.

DSCR rental loans: a 30-year fixed loan from $50,000 to $2 million per property, up to 80 percent of value on a purchase or rate-and-term refinance and 75 percent on a cash-out, qualified on the rent with no minimum credit score. Inside Portland, project rents within the city's stabilization rules.

A Lewiston example: a two-family worth $340,000 after renovation supports up to $238,000 at 70 percent. If purchase and renovation cost $260,000 together, the investor brings about $22,000 plus closing costs and the buyer's half of the transfer tax. Loans are for non-owner-occupied property held in an LLC or corporation, and the lender decides the final terms.

Questions investors ask

Can I get a hard money loan in Maine?

Yes. Our lending partners review non-owner-occupied investment property in Maine held in an LLC or corporation. Most Maine deals are in greater Portland, southern Maine, Lewiston and Auburn, and the Brunswick area. Farther north, ask about the exact address early. The lender decides each deal.

Can I get a fix and flip loan in Bangor?

Ask about the exact address first. Bangor is about two hours from Portland and its market is smaller, so a lender will look closely at the comparable sales and the resale market for that street before deciding.

How much is the transfer tax on a Maine flip?

$2.20 for every $500 of value, split equally between buyer and seller. On a $300,000 sale each side pays about $660, so a flip pays its half twice: once buying and once selling. Transfers above $1 million pay a higher rate since November 2025.

How much notice does a Maine landlord need to give for a rent increase?

At least 45 days' written notice, and 75 days if the increase is 10 percent or more, counting smaller increases within a year together. Portland also has its own rent stabilization rules.

Can a Maine landlord charge a late fee?

Only after rent is 15 days late, and the fee cannot be more than 4 percent of the monthly rent. It must be disclosed in writing when the tenancy begins.

How long does a Maine borrower have to redeem after foreclosure?

For mortgages signed since October 1, 1975, the owner has 90 days after the foreclosure judgment to pay what the court finds due and keep the property.
