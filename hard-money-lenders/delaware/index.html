Hard Money Lenders in Delaware: What the Numbers Need to Include

In Delaware, the transfer tax can cost a flip more than the property tax ever will.

Send us your Delaware dealRun the flip numbers

Every county sits inside a larger metro area, property taxes are low, and there is no sales tax. But the realty transfer tax is among the highest in the country, and recent reassessments have changed tax bills. This page covers what that means for a flip or a rental, the state's landlord rules, and our lending partners' terms.

Three counties, three markets

Delaware is small, and every county sits inside a larger metropolitan area, so location rarely decides a Delaware deal on its own. Cost does.

New Castle County, Wilmington, Newark, Middletown and the northern suburbs, is part of the Philadelphia metro area, with the state's oldest housing stock and its deepest rental demand.

Kent County, Dover and its surroundings, is the state capital and home to Dover Air Force Base, with lower prices and a government and military tenant base.

Sussex County, from Georgetown and Seaford to the beach towns, is part of the Salisbury, Maryland metro area. Inland Sussex and the resort towns are different markets: price the coast on beach-area comparable sales and check local rental rules before you count on seasonal income.

Large rural parcels in Kent and Sussex are harder to value than subdivision homes, so send the address early and the lender will say whether it fits.

The transfer tax is the number to watch

Delaware's property taxes are low by East Coast standards, but its realty transfer tax is among the highest in the country: typically 4 percent of the price in combined state and local tax, customarily split between buyer and seller.

For a flipper that changes the math more than almost any other Delaware rule. Buy for $250,000 and sell for $350,000, with the tax split the customary way, and your share is about $5,000 on the purchase and $7,000 on the sale: roughly $12,000 in transfer tax on one flip. In a state with a 0.1 percent tax the same flip would pay about $600.

Put the transfer tax in your numbers on day one, next to commissions, and check the contract to see who is paying what. It is negotiable, and on a flip, where you are both the buyer and the seller, it is worth negotiating on both ends.

Delaware's landlord rules

The Delaware Residential Landlord-Tenant Code applies statewide.

Security deposits: no more than one month's rent on a lease of a year or more, except for a furnished unit. The deposit must be held in a disclosed, federally insured escrow account and returned, with an itemized list of any deductions, within 20 days after the lease ends. Missing the deadline can cost double the amount withheld.

Rent increases: at least 60 days' written notice, and the tenant has 15 days to respond.

Unpaid rent: after the grace period, a five-day notice to pay or quit before filing for eviction, and late fees capped at 5 percent of the monthly rent.

Ending a month-to-month tenancy: 60 days' written notice.

These are more tenant-protective than in many Southern and Midwestern states, but they are clear and statewide, which makes them easy to plan around.

Property taxes: check the new numbers

Delaware's counties recently completed their first general reassessments in decades, and the new values now drive tax bills. A tax figure copied from an old listing, or from a website that has not caught up, may not match what the property will actually owe. Pull the current assessment and the latest bill from the county, and add the school district rate, before you estimate holding costs on a rental.

Delaware also forecloses through the courts, with a sheriff's sale at the end, a slower process than the trustee sales used in many states. For an investor buying distressed property, that means long timelines and the need for a title company to confirm the court process was completed before you close.

Questions investors ask

Can I get a hard money loan in Delaware?

Yes. Our lending partners review deals in all three counties. The property must be a non-owner-occupied investment held in an LLC or corporation, and each deal is sized on its after-repair value, cost and your experience. The lender decides.

How much is the transfer tax on a Delaware flip?

The combined state and local realty transfer tax is typically 4 percent of the price, customarily split between buyer and seller. On a flip you pay your share twice, once buying and once selling, so a $250,000 buy and $350,000 sale can cost about $12,000.

What do hard money lenders charge in Delaware?

Our lending partners' fix-and-flip pricing starts at 12.99 percent plus 2.99 points. Loyalty pricing of 10.99 percent plus 1.99 points can apply after two loans are paid off in good standing. Transfer tax, appraisal, title and closing costs are separate.

How much security deposit can I charge on a Delaware rental?

For a lease of a year or more, no more than one month's rent, except on a furnished unit. Hold it in a disclosed escrow account and return it, with an itemized list of deductions, within 20 days after the lease ends.

How fast can I evict for unpaid rent in Delaware?

After the grace period, a landlord serves a five-day notice to pay or quit before filing in the Justice of the Peace Court. Late fees are capped at 5 percent of the monthly rent.

Did Delaware property taxes change after reassessment?

Yes. All three counties completed their first general reassessments in decades, and the new values now drive tax bills, so an older tax figure on a listing may be wrong. Check the county's current assessment and bill before you estimate holding costs.
