Hard Money Lenders in Mississippi: Where Financing Works and How to Price a Deal

Mississippi has low prices and no transfer tax, but it keeps sale prices private and taxes rentals at a higher ratio than homes.

Where Mississippi investors work

Mississippi has low house prices, no transfer tax and quick landlord remedies. It also keeps sale prices out of the public record, assesses a rental at a higher ratio than an owner's home, and on the coast prices wind insurance separately. This page covers each one, then our lending partners' terms.

The Jackson metro: Jackson, Madison, Ridgeland, Brandon, Flowood, Pearl and Clinton in Hinds, Rankin and Madison counties. The Gulf Coast: Gulfport, Biloxi, Ocean Springs and D'Iberville, with Pascagoula and Moss Point farther east. DeSoto County: Southaven, Olive Branch, Horn Lake and Hernando, which sit in the Memphis metro and share its buyers and renters.

Where it gets harder: Hattiesburg, Tupelo, Meridian, Columbus and the Delta are regional centers with smaller markets, and many very low-priced houses there fall below the $50,000 minimum loan. Private lenders also look harder at property with few recent comparable sales. Ask about the exact address before you commit money.

Pricing a flip where sale prices are private

Mississippi is a non-disclosure state. Sale prices are not generally recorded in a way the public can search, so the tools investors elsewhere use to check a value, public records and automated estimates, are much weaker here.

That matters most for a flip, because the after-repair value sets both the size of the loan and the size of the profit. Build it from closed sales pulled from the multiple listing service by a local agent who works the neighborhood, and ask how each comparable was finished. The lender's appraiser will be working from the same kind of data, so a value you can support with real closed sales is the one likely to hold up.

Taxes: what helps and what does not

No transfer tax. Mississippi does not tax the transfer of real estate, so a flip keeps money that would go to the state in many others.

Rentals are assessed at 15 percent, homes at 10. Mississippi's constitution sorts property into classes. A single-family home occupied by its owner as a homestead is Class I, assessed at 10 percent of true value; other real property, including a rental house, is Class II, assessed at 15 percent. The owner-occupant also gets the homestead exemption. Buy a house as a rental and the assessment rises by half before the millage is applied, so recompute the seller's bill before you size a DSCR loan.

Foreclosure by trustee sale. Mississippi forecloses outside court under a deed of trust, with no statutory right to redeem after the sale, a faster and more certain process than the judicial foreclosure used in some states.

Mississippi landlord rules

Unpaid rent: under the Mississippi Residential Landlord and Tenant Act, a landlord can give written notice that the agreement will end if rent is not paid within three days. Evictions go through justice or county court. If a court grants possession based only on unpaid rent, the tenant can still stop the removal by paying everything owed by the court's move-out date.

Deposits: Mississippi does not cap the size of a security deposit. It must be returned within 45 days after the tenancy ends, the tenant gives back possession and asks for it, with an itemized list of any deductions.

No local rent control. Rents are set by the market and the lease.

Insurance on the Gulf Coast

If your deal is in Hancock, Harrison or Jackson County, price the insurance before anything else. Many coastal homes cannot get wind coverage in the regular market and are written for wind by the Mississippi Windstorm Underwriting Association, the state's insurer of last resort for the coastal counties, alongside a separate policy for everything else. Some properties need flood coverage too. Every lender requires the property to be insured, so get real quotes during your inspection period. A Gulf Coast rental that works at inland insurance prices may not work at coastal ones.

Questions investors ask

Can I get a hard money loan in Mississippi?

Yes. Our lending partners review non-owner-occupied investment property in Mississippi, held in an LLC or corporation, most often around Jackson, on the Gulf Coast and in DeSoto County. Check that the loan you need clears the $50,000 minimum. The lender decides each deal.

Why is the property tax higher on a Mississippi rental?

An owner-occupied home is assessed at 10 percent of true value and gets the homestead exemption. A rental is Class II property assessed at 15 percent with no homestead exemption, so the same house carries a noticeably larger bill once it is a rental.

Does Mississippi have a real estate transfer tax?

No. Mississippi does not charge a transfer tax when property is sold, which keeps more of a flip's margin than in states that tax the purchase and the resale.

How do I find comps in Mississippi if sale prices are private?

Use an agent or appraiser with multiple listing service access to pull closed sales of finished homes near your property. Public records and automated estimates are thin in a non-disclosure state.

How does a Mississippi landlord handle unpaid rent?

The landlord can give written notice that the rental agreement ends if rent is not paid within three days. If a court later grants possession only for unpaid rent, the tenant can still stay by paying everything owed by the move-out date the court sets.

How hard is it to insure a rental on the Mississippi coast?

Harder than inland. Many coastal homes get wind coverage through the Mississippi Windstorm Underwriting Association plus a separate policy for other perils, and some need flood insurance. Get quotes during your inspection period.
