Two rule changes Louisiana investors need to know

Louisiana changed two of the rules investors lean on most, and many have not caught up. A parish tax sale no longer sells you the house. And insurers must discount the hurricane premium on homes with a FORTIFIED designation from January 1, 2027, in a state where insurance can decide whether a deal works at all. Here is how to underwrite a Louisiana deal around both, whether you need a hard money loan, a private money loan or a DSCR loan.

The tax sale now sells a lien, not the property

For decades, a Louisiana tax sale transferred an ownership interest to the winning bidder, subject to redemption. That changed on January 1, 2026. Voters approved the constitutional amendment in Act 409 of 2024 on December 7, 2024, and Act 774 of 2024 supplied the rules. Under the new system, the parish auctions a tax lien certificate for the taxes owed, and the delinquent owner keeps the land.

The owner has three years from the recording of the certificate to pay the taxes and extinguish the lien. If it is not paid, the lien holder can enforce it through a court process that ends in a sale, with any surplus going back to the former owner.

For a hard money deal, the lesson is simple. Buying at a Louisiana tax sale is now a years-long lien investment, not a way to acquire a house to renovate next month, and a renovation loan needs title to the property as security. If the goal is a property to fix and sell, buy the property itself, from an owner, at a sheriff's sale that delivers title, or from a parish selling adjudicated property, and get a title company's written view before you commit.

Insurance decides Louisiana deals, and the FORTIFIED rules just changed

A Louisiana investor's biggest carrying cost is often the wind policy, not the loan. In a March 2025 report the Louisiana Legislative Auditor found that homeowners who received a state Fortify Homes grant and installed a FORTIFIED roof saw the median annual premium fall from about $5,625 to $4,375, a saving of about 22 percent.

Three separate programs sit around that number, and investors need to know which apply to them.

The grant. The Louisiana Fortify Homes Program pays up to $10,000 toward a FORTIFIED roof for owner-occupied homes. A flip or a rental does not qualify.

The tax credit. A state income tax credit of up to $10,000 covers FORTIFIED roof work paid on or after July 1, 2025. It too is limited to owner-occupied homes with a homestead exemption, and the credits are capped at $10 million a fiscal year.

The insurance discount. This is the one that follows the house. Under the Department of Insurance's Regulation 136, announced in Bulletin 2026-04, insurers must apply discounts set from a Louisiana-specific benchmark table to residential policies on FORTIFIED homes, for new and renewal policies issued on or after January 1, 2027. A FORTIFIED designation stays with the house, so an investor who builds to the standard at their own cost hands the next buyer a lower premium to point at, and a landlord keeps a lower carrying cost.

Get the wind and flood quotes on the exact address before the offer. If the property is in a special flood hazard area, flood insurance is part of the monthly cost, not an option.

The property tax a rental pays, not the one the seller paid

Louisiana assesses residential property at 10 percent of its fair market value, and the homestead exemption removes the first $75,000 of value ($7,500 of assessed value) for an owner who lives in the home. A rental gets no homestead exemption. On a modest house that exemption can wipe out most of the seller's bill, so the tax an investor will pay can be many times what the listing shows.

Parishes reappraise property on a four-year cycle. Underwrite a rental on 10 percent of the value you expect after renovation, with no exemption, times the millage for that address.

New Orleans and the rest of Louisiana

New Orleans is the state's highest-priced market, and its averages can mislead. The New Orleans Metropolitan Association of Realtors reported an average August 2026 sale price of $369,344 across its metro, up 8.7 percent, and $493,953 in Orleans Parish alone, up 16.4 percent. An average is pulled up by a few expensive sales; neither figure is an after-repair value for your street.

In New Orleans, flood zone and elevation can change a house's insurable cost more than its neighborhood does. Check the flood zone and get an elevation certificate if one exists before pricing a renovation.

Baton Rouge, Shreveport and Lafayette are the other investor markets. Each prices differently, and Shreveport's lower values make the $50,000 minimum loan the first thing to check on a cheap house.

DSCR loans in Louisiana

A DSCR loan qualifies the property rather than your income: the rent has to cover the payment, including principal, interest, taxes and insurance. Our lending partners' DSCR program lends from $75,000 to $2 million, up to 80 percent of value on a purchase or rate-and-term refinance, with no minimum credit score.

In Louisiana the calculation turns on insurance. The same rent that covers a payment in Shreveport may not cover it in a coastal parish once wind and flood premiums are in. That is why a FORTIFIED roof and the 2027 discount rules matter to a buy-and-hold investor: every dollar off the premium is a dollar of debt service coverage.

Fix and flip loans in Louisiana: how the loan gets sized

Fix and flip, rehab and bridge pricing starts at 12.99 percent plus 2.99 points. Borrowers who have paid off two loans with the lending partner in good standing can qualify for loyalty pricing of 10.99 percent plus 1.99 points. Loans start at $50,000, the minimum credit score is 600, and the loan can cover up to 100 percent of cost but no more than 70 percent of after-repair value, whichever binds first. Leverage depends on the projects you can document from the past three years. Property must be non-owner-occupied and held in an LLC or corporation.

A Baton Rouge example: a house worth $240,000 after renovation supports up to $168,000 at 70 percent. If purchase and renovation cost $180,000 together, the investor brings about $12,000 plus closing costs and the insurance premiums. The lending partner's team decides the final terms on every deal.

Six things to check before a Louisiana offer

1. Get wind and flood quotes on the exact address. In many parishes they are the biggest carrying cost.

2. If the roof is being replaced, price the FORTIFIED upgrade. The designation stays with the house, and the 2027 discounts follow it.

3. Recalculate the property tax without the homestead exemption, at 10 percent of the value after renovation.

4. Do not plan a flip around a tax sale. Since 2026 it sells a lien with a three-year redemption period.

5. Get written contractor bids, include the roof and any elevation or flood work, and hold back at least ten percent.

6. If you plan to rent, run the DSCR with the real premiums.

What gets a Louisiana deal declined

A tax sale purchase. A lien certificate is not title, and it cannot secure a renovation loan.

No insurance quote. Wind and flood can decide whether a rental covers its payment.

A tax figure that includes the seller's homestead exemption. A rental does not get it.

A camp, second home or house for family. Property you or a close relative will live in is owner-occupied, and our lending partners do not finance it.

Questions investors ask

How much do hard money loans cost in Louisiana?

Our lending partners' fix and flip and bridge pricing starts at 12.99 percent plus 2.99 points. Borrowers who have paid off two loans with the lending partner in good standing can qualify for 10.99 percent plus 1.99 points. Appraisal, title and closing costs are extra, and wind and flood insurance are carrying costs to budget alongside the loan.

Can I get a DSCR loan on a New Orleans rental?

Yes. The DSCR program runs from $75,000 to $2 million, up to 80 percent of value on a purchase, with no minimum credit score. In Louisiana the insurance premium is usually the number that decides whether the rent covers the payment.

Can I use a hard money loan to buy at a Louisiana tax sale?

No. Since January 1, 2026, a Louisiana tax sale sells a tax lien certificate, not the property, and the owner has three years to pay and clear the lien. A renovation loan needs title to the property as security.

Does a FORTIFIED roof lower insurance on a rental in Louisiana?

From January 1, 2027, Regulation 136 requires insurers to discount residential policies on FORTIFIED homes, and that applies to the house, not the owner. The state grant and the income tax credit, by contrast, are for owner-occupied homes with a homestead exemption.

Why is the property tax on my Louisiana rental higher than the seller's?

The seller likely had the homestead exemption, which removes the first $75,000 of value from tax for an owner who lives there. A rental does not get it, so you pay on the full 10 percent assessment.

Are private money lenders different from hard money lenders?

Not in any way that changes the loan. Investors use both names for short-term, asset-based financing from non-bank lenders, secured by investment property, and the same program terms apply whichever you searched for.
