The tax line that catches out-of-state buyers

Michigan has a reputation as cheap Midwest cash flow, and in parts of the state the prices support it. But the tax bill a buyer inherits is almost never the one printed on the listing, for two separate reasons. Start here, because it is the most expensive thing to learn late.

1. The rental loses the Principal Residence Exemption. A house lived in by its owner carries the Principal Residence Exemption under MCL 211.7cc, which exempts it from up to 18 mills of local school operating tax. A rental never qualifies, so a landlord pays those mills on top of everything the owner-occupant paid. The PRE is not the same thing as the Homestead Property Tax Credit claimed on a state income tax return; it is the PRE that a rental loses.

2. The taxable value uncaps after the sale. Since Proposal A in 1994, Michigan taxes property on its taxable value, and increases are capped each year at the lower of inflation or 5 percent while the same owner holds it. When the property transfers, the cap comes off: under MCL 211.27a the taxable value resets to the state equalized value, half of true cash value, in the year after the sale. A seller who has owned a house for twenty years may be paying tax on a taxable value far below half of what you are paying for it.

Put the two together and the first full bill on a long-held house bought as a rental can be a multiple of the seller's. Estimate it as half of the price you expect the assessor to accept, times the full non-homestead millage for that parcel, and use that figure in a DSCR ratio. Michigan has a working assessment appeal process through the March Board of Review, but you argue from the correct starting number.

Closing costs and foreclosure in Michigan

Transfer taxes. Michigan charges a state real estate transfer tax of $3.75 per $500 of value and a county transfer tax, 55 cents per $500 in most counties, together about 0.86 percent. By custom the seller pays them, so on a flip you meet them at the resale: about $2,150 on a $250,000 sale.

Foreclosure by advertisement, with redemption. Most Michigan mortgages are foreclosed outside court by advertisement and a sheriff's sale. The former owner then has a redemption period, commonly six months for a residential property, under MCL 600.3240, with shorter or longer periods in specific cases. A buyer at a sheriff's sale receives a deed that becomes absolute only when that period expires, so a sheriff's sale is a poor fit for a quick renovation loan.

Tax foreclosure is separate. County treasurers foreclose on tax-delinquent property and sell it at auction, and since the Michigan Supreme Court's 2020 Rafaeli decision former owners can claim surplus proceeds. Get a title company's view before buying any property that came through a tax foreclosure.

Michigan landlord law, in short

Unpaid rent: a seven-day demand for possession before filing in district court (MCL 600.5714). Evictions run through the district courts; in Detroit that is the 36th District Court.

Security deposits: capped at one and a half months' rent (MCL 554.602), and the landlord must mail an itemized list of damages within 30 days after the tenancy ends or return the deposit.

No rent control. State law bars local governments from controlling rents on private residential property.

Local rental rules. Detroit requires rental properties to be registered and to pass inspection for a Certificate of Compliance, and many other cities, Grand Rapids and Lansing among them, run their own rental certification. Budget both the cost and the calendar time before a unit can legally produce income.

Michigan's markets are different businesses

Detroit has the lowest entry prices and the highest gross yields, and the highest execution risk. Low prices mean the $50,000 minimum loan is the first thing to check, older housing means a real capital budget, and the city's rental registration and inspection add calendar time. Price the exit from renovated comparable sales on the same blocks, not a citywide average.

The inner-ring suburbs, Royal Oak, Ferndale, Berkley and their neighbors, cost more and yield less, but carry strong schools, lower tenant turnover and an exit that includes owner-occupant buyers. For a first Michigan rental financed on DSCR, they are often the most forgiving.

Grand Rapids and West Michigan behave more like a value-add and appreciation market than a cash flow one at median prices. Ann Arbor runs on University of Michigan demand. Lansing, Flint and Kalamazoo sit in between on price.

What gets a Michigan deal declined

Taxes taken from the seller's bill. The signature Michigan error: no PRE on a rental, and an uncapped taxable value the year after the sale.

A Detroit after-repair value built on a citywide average. Block-level comparable sales are the only defensible basis for an exit price.

A sheriff's sale purchase planned as a quick flip. The redemption period has to run before the deed becomes absolute.

An unregistered rental. Registration and inspection are required in Detroit and many other cities.

A primary residence. Our lending partners do not finance a home you intend to live in.

Questions investors ask

Can I get a hard money loan in Detroit?

Yes. Our lending partners review non-owner-occupied investment property in Detroit and across Michigan, held in an LLC or corporation. On low-priced Detroit houses, check that the loan you need clears the $50,000 minimum. The lender decides each deal.

Why will my Michigan property tax be higher than the seller's?

Two reasons. A rental does not get the Principal Residence Exemption, so it pays up to 18 school operating mills the owner-occupant did not. And the taxable value uncaps the year after a sale, resetting to half of market value.

What is the transfer tax on a Michigan sale?

The state tax is $3.75 per $500 and the county tax is 55 cents per $500 in most counties, about 0.86 percent in total, customarily paid by the seller.

Can I flip a house I bought at a Michigan sheriff's sale?

Not quickly. After a mortgage foreclosure sale the former owner usually has a redemption period, commonly six months for a home, and your deed only becomes absolute when it ends.

How much security deposit can a Michigan landlord charge?

Up to one and a half months' rent. The landlord must return it or mail an itemized list of damages within 30 days after the tenancy ends.

Does Detroit require rental registration?

Yes. Detroit rentals must be registered and pass inspection for a Certificate of Compliance, so put the inspection and any repairs into the timeline before you count on rent.
