The widest flip margins of any big metro

If there is a large American market where the 2026 flip numbers still look like the old days, it is Pittsburgh. ATTOM's report on the second quarter of 2026 put the typical gross flipping margin in the Pittsburgh metro at 81.5 percent, the highest of any large metro in the country, ahead of Buffalo and New Orleans, and nearly four times the national figure of 21.5 percent. Low purchase prices, old but solid housing stock and steady demand from buyers who cannot find renovated homes make the spread.

Gross margin is measured before renovation, points, interest, taxes and commissions, and a high percentage on a low purchase price is still a modest dollar figure. Three local facts keep it honest: a 5 percent transfer tax, assessments that can jump after you buy, and a cheap end of the market where the loan minimum starts to bind.

A 5 percent transfer tax

Within the City of Pittsburgh, the realty transfer tax is 5 percent of the price: 3 percent for the city, 1 percent for the Pittsburgh School District and 1 percent for Pennsylvania. It has been at that level since 2020, it is among the highest in the country, and by custom it is split between buyer and seller.

On a flip bought for $90,000 and sold for $210,000, your half is about $2,250 at purchase and $5,250 at resale: roughly $7,500 in transfer tax. Outside the city, in the suburban municipalities, rates are usually lower. Allegheny County publishes the rate for each municipality and school district; check it before you make an offer.

Why your tax bill can change after you buy

Allegheny County still assesses property on 2012 base-year values, so many houses carry assessments far below today's market and the tax bill on a listing can look very low.

Pennsylvania lets school districts appeal assessments, and a recent sale is the usual trigger. In an appeal, the sale price is converted into an assessment using the state's common level ratio for the county. The ratio was 50.14 percent for appeals through June 2026, and the State Tax Equalization Board's new ratio for the 2027 tax year, in use from July 2026, is about 49.3 percent. At roughly half, a house bought for $200,000 can end up assessed near $100,000, which may be far above the old figure.

Two 2026 changes to build in. The City of Pittsburgh raised its property tax rate for 2026 from 8.06 mills to 9.67 mills, a 20 percent increase. And on August 17, 2026, a court ordered Allegheny County to carry out its first countywide reassessment since 2012, starting by 2027 and finishing within five years. A rental you hold for several years will very likely be taxed on a new value before you sell it.

For a buy-and-hold investor, estimate the tax as if the district will appeal, add the new city millage, and size a DSCR loan on that. For a flipper it matters less, but your buyer's lender will look at the same numbers.

The cheap end of the market

Pittsburgh is one of the few big metros where very low prices create a financing problem. Our lending partners' short-term loans start at $50,000 and are capped at 70 percent of after-repair value, so a renovated house needs to be worth roughly $72,000 or more to support even the minimum loan. Many Pittsburgh houses sell for well under that before renovation, and on some streets even a finished house does not clear it.

Build the after-repair value from renovated sales on the same street. If they sit below that level, the deal is a cash purchase however cheap it looks, and the resale market on that street is thinner than the metro margin suggests.

Questions Pittsburgh investors ask

Can I get a hard money loan to flip a house in Pittsburgh?

Yes, for non-owner-occupied investment property. Our lending partners' short-term loans start at $50,000 with a 600 minimum credit score, priced from 12.99 percent plus 2.99 points, up to 100 percent of cost and 70 percent of after-repair value. The lender decides each file.

Is Pittsburgh a good place to flip houses in 2026?

On the numbers it is the strongest large market in the country. ATTOM put the typical gross flipping margin in the Pittsburgh metro at 81.5 percent in the second quarter of 2026, the highest of any large metro. Gross margin is before renovation, financing, taxes and selling costs.

How much is the transfer tax in Pittsburgh?

Within the city, 5 percent of the price: 3 percent for the city, 1 percent for the Pittsburgh School District and 1 percent for the state. It is customarily split between buyer and seller. Suburban rates are usually lower.

Why might my Pittsburgh property taxes go up after I buy?

Allegheny County still uses 2012 values, and school districts can appeal after a sale, converting your price into an assessment with the common level ratio, about 49.3 percent for 2027 tax year appeals (50.14 percent the year before). The city also raised its rate to 9.67 mills for 2026, and a countywide reassessment has been ordered.

Is a $60,000 Pittsburgh house too cheap to finance?

It can be. The minimum loan is $50,000 and the loan is capped at 70 percent of after-repair value, so the finished house generally needs to be worth about $72,000 or more. Check renovated sales on the same street first.

When will Allegheny County reassess?

A court order in August 2026 requires the county to start a countywide reassessment by 2027 and finish it within five years, then reassess every five years after that. Until then, 2012 values and appeals set most bills.
