Hard Money Lenders in Atlanta

Atlanta flipped more of its houses than any other big metro in early 2026, and its flips still paid.

It is also a market that has turned unevenly, and the investors who do well here in 2026 are the ones who can tell which half of the metro they are standing in.

The most-flipped big market in the country

ATTOM's first-quarter 2026 flipping report put metro Atlanta's flipping rate at 12.3 percent of all home sales, the highest of any metro area with a million or more people. Nationally the figure was 8 percent. In ATTOM's second-quarter report, released at the start of October 2026, flipping cooled nationally to 6.2 percent of sales and Atlanta was no longer among the five most-flipped large metros, a reminder that the pace here moves quarter to quarter.

The returns have held up better than the national headlines suggest. A special analysis by ATTOM and Backflip of first-quarter 2026 loan-level deals found the average Atlanta flip was bought for about $370,000, resold for about $470,000, and produced a gross profit near $100,000, a 27 percent gross return, with loans paid off in roughly 90 days. Dallas-Fort Worth, measured the same way, averaged a 4.3 percent gross return.

Two cautions before anyone reads that as an invitation. Gross profit is before holding costs, financing, closing costs and commissions, so the net is materially smaller. And a high flipping rate cuts both ways: the houses you are competing to buy are being competed for by more investors than almost anywhere else, which is why disciplined offers matter more here, not less.

What is an Atlanta house worth? It depends who you ask

This matters because the after-repair value is the number your loan is built on, and the published Atlanta figures genuinely disagree:

FMLS, July 2026: median detached home $475,000, up 2.2 percent on a year earlier.
Metro figures for August 2026: a median sale price near $395,000, down about 1.3 percent on a year earlier, with 5.4 months of supply and a median of 59 days on market.

Neither is wrong. They measure different things: detached houses against all homes, one month's closings against another's, one set of counties against another. The spread between them, roughly $80,000, is close to the whole gross profit on a typical Atlanta flip.

So an Atlanta ARV cannot come from a metro median, a ZIP-code average or an online estimate. It comes from closed sales of comparable, renovated houses within a short distance of the subject property, sold recently. A lender's valuation will be built that way whether yours is or not, and the gap between the two is where deals get cut.

The turn is real, and it is uneven

The same FMLS report that shows detached prices still rising shows the other half of the market weakening. Townhomes and condominiums, what FMLS calls attached homes, had 6.5 months of supply in July 2026, up from 6.0 a year earlier. That is buyer's-market territory.

For a flip, that means two practical adjustments. On attached product, budget for a longer sale than the 2021 to 2023 market taught everyone to expect, and price the exit off what has actually closed, not what is listed. In the lower-priced parts of the metro, where investor competition is heaviest, check whether your comps include recent bulk or institutional sales that are setting prices lower than older data shows.

There is also a longer-term factor. Under the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act, signed in July 2026, entities with investment control of 350 or more single-family homes cannot buy another from January 7, 2027, subject to exceptions that include some renovation and new-construction activity. Metro Atlanta is one of the markets where those buyers held a meaningful share. What that does to Atlanta prices will vary street by street, and anyone promising a citywide effect is guessing. How the loan gets sized on an Atlanta flip

Our lending partners' fix-and-flip and bridge programs lend up to 70 percent of after-repair value and up to 100 percent of cost (purchase plus rehab), whichever limit is reached first, with a minimum loan of $50,000 and a minimum credit score of 600. Standard pricing starts at 12.99 percent plus 2.99 points. Borrowers who have paid off two loans with the lending partner in good standing can qualify for loyalty pricing of 10.99 percent plus 1.99 points. How much leverage you get depends on the experience you can document over the past three years, and the lender decides.

Run the Atlanta averages above through those limits and the practical point is obvious. At a $470,000 resale value, 70 percent is about $329,000: less than the $370,000 average purchase price before a dollar of renovation. A typical Atlanta flip therefore needs real cash from the investor, and the size of that check, not the interest rate, is usually what decides whether the deal happens.

Time is the other cost. On a $300,000 loan at 12.99 percent, every additional month of interest is about $3,250. The 90-day Atlanta average is an average; a townhome that sits for five months on the market turns a thin deal into a losing one. Build the budget on the slower timeline and let a fast sale be the bonus.

Loans are for non-owner-occupied investment property only, held in an entity such as an LLC or corporation. Limits can differ by property and county, so confirm them with the lender for your specific address.

"No credit check" is the wrong question

People search for hard money lenders in Atlanta with no credit check, and the honest answer is that there is no such loan through our lending partners. Credit is pulled, and the minimum score is 600.

What is true is that credit is not what decides the file. These loans are asset-based: the property, the purchase price, the renovation budget and the exit carry the decision. A borrower at 640 with a well-bought house and a credible exit is a stronger file than one at 780 with an optimistic ARV. What tends to hurt a file is a recent bankruptcy, foreclosure or short sale, not a middling score.

Before you make an offer in metro Atlanta

1. Name the county, then the product. A detached house in Cherokee and a townhome in Clayton are different markets that happen to share a metro. Decide which one you are underwriting before you look at a single number.

2. Pull three to five renovated comps, sold in the last six months, close to the property. Same product type, similar size, similar finish. If you cannot find them, that is information about your exit, not a gap to fill with an online estimate.

3. Check what is listed against you. With 5.4 months of supply metro-wide, your resale competes with every similar house for sale that month. Count them, and note how long they have been sitting.

4. Get contractor bids, not estimates. Add a contingency of at least ten percent for what you will find behind the walls of an older house.

5. Price the holding period at five or six months, not three. Interest, taxes, insurance and utilities for every month the property is yours. If the deal only works at 90 days, it does not work.

6. Get a real insurance quote on the specific address during diligence. Vacant-property and builder's-risk coverage costs more than a standard homeowner policy, and it belongs in the budget before you commit.

7. Know your exit before you buy. Sell or rent. If there is any chance you will hold it, check what it would rent for and whether that rent supports a DSCR refinance at the value you expect.

What gets an Atlanta deal declined

An ARV from a metro median. Published Atlanta medians disagree by about $80,000. Only nearby renovated comps count.

A townhome budgeted on a 2022 timeline. Attached inventory is at six-plus months of supply. The holding costs have to reflect that.

A rehab budget without contractor bids. In a market this busy with investors, trades are booked and prices move. Written bids, not estimates.

No reserves. The lender needs to see cash beyond the down payment and renovation to carry the loan if the sale runs long.

A home you plan to live in. Our lending partners finance non-owner-occupied investment property only, held in an entity. An owner-occupied home is a consumer mortgage, which is a different product.

Atlanta hard money questions

Can I get a hard money loan in Atlanta with no credit check?

Not through our lending partners. Credit is pulled and the minimum score on fix-and-flip and bridge programs is 600. It affects pricing and eligibility, but the property, the budget and the exit carry the decision.

How much do hard money lenders charge in Atlanta?

Our lending partners' standard fix-and-flip pricing starts at 12.99 percent interest plus 2.99 points. Borrowers who have paid off two loans with the lending partner in good standing can qualify for 10.99 percent plus 1.99 points. Appraisal, title and closing costs are additional, and the lender sets the final terms.

How much cash do I need to flip a house in Atlanta?

More than many first-time flippers expect. Loans are capped at 70 percent of after-repair value, so on the average Atlanta flip in ATTOM and Backflip's first-quarter 2026 sample ($370,000 in, $470,000 out) the loan limit is about $329,000, below the purchase price alone. You bring the gap, closing costs and reserves.

Do Atlanta hard money lenders require an appraisal?

Expect the lender to order a valuation of the property, usually an appraisal, including an after-repair value for a renovation loan. The loan is sized on that figure, not on your estimate, so build your ARV from nearby renovated sales.

Are Atlanta flips still profitable in 2026?

The average one was in early 2026: ATTOM and Backflip found a 27 percent gross return on first-quarter Atlanta flips, against 4.3 percent in Dallas-Fort Worth. That is before holding, financing and selling costs, and attached homes are now selling more slowly, so the margin on any single deal depends on the purchase price and the product.

How do I refinance out of a hard money loan on an Atlanta rental?

The usual exit is a DSCR loan, which qualifies on the property's rent rather than your personal income. Our lending partners' 30-year fixed DSCR program goes up to 80 percent of value on a purchase or rate-and-term refinance and 75 percent on a cash-out refinance. The rent and the appraised value at refinance decide how much of your cash comes back out.
