Hard Money Lenders in Wisconsin: What to Plan For

Wisconsin runs one landlord code from Milwaukee to Madison, with short notice periods and no local rent control.

Send us your Wisconsin dealSee DSCR rental loans

The catches are high property taxes that rentals carry in full, a judicial foreclosure process with a redemption period, and winters that slow exterior work. Here is how it all fits, and what our lending partners offer.

Where Wisconsin investors are buying

Milwaukee and the southeast: Milwaukee's duplexes and two-flats are the state's classic rental product, with Waukesha, Racine and Kenosha around it and Kenosha also drawing on the Chicago commuter market.

Madison and Dane County: state government and the University of Wisconsin keep rental demand deep and vacancy low, at higher prices.

The Fox Valley and Green Bay: Green Bay, Appleton, Neenah, Oshkosh and Fond du Lac, with a manufacturing and paper-industry base.

The regional centers: Eau Claire, La Crosse and Wausau have smaller resale markets and, in the Northwoods beyond them, far fewer comparable sales. Build the after-repair value carefully there and expect a slower exit.

Wisconsin's landlord rules are fast and statewide

Wisconsin sets landlord-tenant rules in state statute and administrative code (ATCP 134), and state law bars cities, villages, towns and counties from regulating the amount of rent, so the rules are the same in Milwaukee as in Madison.

Unpaid rent. For a month-to-month tenancy or a lease of one year or less, the landlord can serve a five-day notice to pay or vacate. If the tenant pays, the tenancy continues. A second nonpayment within twelve months under a lease allows a 14-day notice with no chance to cure. Leases longer than a year require 30 days.

Ending a month-to-month tenancy takes at least 28 days' written notice.

Security deposits must be returned within 21 days after the tenant leaves, with an itemized list of deductions. ATCP 134 spells out what a landlord may deduct and what must be disclosed at the start of a tenancy, including a check-in sheet the tenant can use to note existing damage.

Evictions run through small claims court. For a buy-and-hold investor, Wisconsin is one of the more predictable states in the Midwest to be a landlord in.

What Wisconsin adds to your costs

Transfer fee. Wisconsin's real estate transfer fee is $3 per $1,000 of value, 0.3 percent, normally paid by the seller. On a $250,000 resale that is $750, modest next to many states.

Property tax. Wisconsin property taxes are among the higher ones in the Midwest, set locally and varying widely by municipality. The lottery and gaming credit applies only to an owner's primary residence, so a rental never gets it. Get the actual tax bill and the local mill rate before you estimate holding costs, and model them on your price, not the seller's assessment.

Winter. A flip that runs through a Wisconsin winter carries heat for a vacant house and a pause on exterior work. A project bought in the fall with a roof or siding in the plan should count on spring for that work, and on a longer hold.

Foreclosure takes time in Wisconsin

Wisconsin foreclosures go through the circuit courts. After judgment, the borrower has a redemption period before the sheriff's sale can happen: its length depends on the type of property, when the mortgage was signed and whether the lender gives up any deficiency claim. A 2016 law (2015 Wisconsin Act 376) cut the periods in half for mortgages signed on or after April 27, 2016: on an owner-occupied one-to-four family home, generally six months, or three months if the lender waives a deficiency, compared with twelve and six months under older mortgages. It is shorter still if the court finds the property abandoned, and the sale must then be confirmed by the court. Ask a Wisconsin attorney which period applies before you plan around a foreclosure.

For an investor buying at a sheriff's sale, that means a long, visible pipeline and a sale that is not final until confirmation. For a lender, it is part of why the exit plan is weighed so heavily on every Wisconsin deal.

Questions investors ask

Can I get a hard money loan in Wisconsin?

Yes. Our lending partners review non-owner-occupied investment property in Wisconsin, from Milwaukee and Madison to the Fox Valley. The property must be held in an entity, and the lender decides each deal on its numbers.

How much is the real estate transfer fee in Wisconsin?

$3 for every $1,000 of value, or 0.3 percent, normally paid by the seller. On a $250,000 resale that is $750.

How much notice does a Wisconsin landlord give for unpaid rent?

For a month-to-month tenancy or a lease of one year or less, a five-day notice to pay or vacate, which the tenant can cure by paying. A second nonpayment within twelve months under a lease allows a 14-day notice with no chance to cure. Leases longer than a year require 30 days.

Can Wisconsin cities limit rent increases?

No. State law prevents Wisconsin cities, villages, towns and counties from regulating the amount of rent charged for private housing.

How long does a Wisconsin foreclosure take?

Longer than in most states. It is judicial, and after judgment the borrower has a redemption period before the sheriff's sale. On a home under a mortgage signed since April 27, 2016 it is generally six months, or three months if the lender waives a deficiency; older mortgages can carry twelve or six months.

How long does a Wisconsin landlord have to return a security deposit?

Within 21 days after the tenant surrenders the unit, with an itemized list of any deductions.
