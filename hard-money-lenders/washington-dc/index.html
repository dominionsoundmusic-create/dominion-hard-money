Hard Money Lenders in Washington, DC: The Rules That Decide a Deal

In DC the rules decide the deal: two taxes on every sale, a tenant's right to buy before an owner sells, much higher tax rates on vacant houses, rent stabilization and a rental license.

Location is never the problem in DC

Every address in the District sits in the middle of one of the largest metro areas in the country, with deep comparable sales from Anacostia to Chevy Chase. What decides a DC deal instead are rules few other places have: two taxes on every sale, a tenant's right to buy, a vacant property tax, rent stabilization and a rental license. Each one belongs in the numbers before you make an offer.

Two taxes on every sale

DC charges a deed recordation tax and a deed transfer tax on every sale. Each is 1.1 percent of the price on residential transfers under $400,000, and 1.45 percent of the entire amount at $400,000 or more. By custom the buyer pays recordation and the seller pays transfer.

On a flip you are the buyer and then the seller, so you pay one of each. Buy for $550,000 and sell for $800,000: about $7,975 in recordation tax going in and $11,600 in transfer tax coming out, $19,575 in all. That is before commissions, financing and renovation. Put it in the first line of your numbers, not the last.

Tenants can have the right to buy first

DC's Tenant Opportunity to Purchase Act, known as TOPA, gives tenants of many rental properties the right to be offered the chance to buy before the owner sells to anyone else, with notice periods and deadlines that can add weeks or months to a sale. The rules differ for multifamily buildings and single-family homes, and a 2018 change narrowed the rights for many single-family rentals, but not all.

For an investor buying an occupied property, TOPA is a timeline and title question. Confirm with a DC real estate attorney whether the seller has complied, or whether the property is exempt, before you commit to a closing date. For a lender, an unresolved TOPA question is a title problem, so expect it to be asked.

A vacant house is taxed at a higher rate

DC taxes occupied residential property (Class 1) at 85 cents per $100 of assessed value. A building classified as vacant (Class 3) is taxed at $5 per $100, and one classified as blighted (Class 4) at $10 per $100. On a $600,000 house that is the difference between about $5,100 a year and $30,000 or $60,000. A flip sits vacant by design, so this matters.

Exemptions exist, including for properties under active renovation with valid permits, but they have to be claimed and kept current. Before you close on a vacant DC house, find out how it is classified today, what it takes to qualify for an exemption during your renovation, and how long the exemption lasts.

For buy-and-hold investors

Rent stabilization. DC's rent stabilization program covers many units in buildings built before 1976. Exemptions include properties owned by natural persons who own four or fewer rental units in the District, which covers many single-family and small multifamily investors, but the exemption has to be claimed with the Rental Accommodations Division. Check a building's status before you project rents.

A rental license. DC requires a basic business license to rent out residential property, with a housing inspection. Budget time for it before the first tenant.

Deposits and evictions. A DC security deposit may not exceed one month's rent and must be returned, or accounted for in writing, within 45 days after the tenancy ends. Evictions run through the Landlord and Tenant Branch of DC Superior Court and take longer than in most of the region, so a DC rental should be underwritten with a realistic vacancy and collection allowance.

A DSCR loan qualifies a rental on its rent. Remember that the lender counts the full property tax in the payment, so use the post-purchase tax figure.

Our lending partners' terms in DC

Fix and flip, rehab and bridge loans are priced from 12.99 percent plus 2.99 points. Borrowers who have paid off two loans with the lending partner in good standing can qualify for loyalty pricing of 10.99 percent plus 1.99 points. Loans start at $50,000 with a 600 minimum credit score and can cover up to 100 percent of cost and up to 70 percent of after-repair value, whichever binds first, with leverage based on experience documented over the past three years.

DSCR rental loans are 30-year fixed, $50,000 to $2 million per property, up to 80 percent of value on a purchase or rate-and-term refinance and 75 percent on a cash-out, with no minimum credit score.

Using the example above: a rowhouse bought for $550,000 and worth $800,000 after renovation supports up to $560,000 at 70 percent of value. If purchase and renovation together cost $700,000, the investor brings about $140,000 plus the $7,975 recordation tax, closing costs and reserves. Property must be non-owner-occupied and held in an entity such as an LLC, and the lender decides the final terms.

Questions investors ask

How much are DC transfer and recordation taxes on a flip?

Each is 1.1 percent of the price on residential transfers under $400,000 and 1.45 percent of the whole price at $400,000 or more. By custom the buyer pays recordation and the seller pays transfer, so a $550,000 purchase and $800,000 resale cost about $19,575 in all.

What is TOPA in DC and does it affect my purchase?

The Tenant Opportunity to Purchase Act gives tenants of many DC rental properties the right to be offered the chance to buy before the owner sells to someone else. Rights differ for multifamily buildings and single-family homes, so have a DC real estate attorney confirm compliance or an exemption before you contract on an occupied property.

How much higher is the tax on a vacant house in DC?

Much higher: $5 per $100 of assessed value for vacant property and $10 per $100 for blighted property, against 85 cents for an occupied home. Properties under active renovation with permits can qualify for an exemption, but it must be claimed.

Is my DC rental subject to rent control?

Possibly, if the building was built before 1976. Natural persons who own four or fewer rental units in DC can claim an exemption from rent stabilization. Check the property's registration with the Rental Accommodations Division.

Can I get a hard money loan in Washington, DC?

Yes. Our lending partners review non-owner-occupied investment property across the District. The property must be held in an entity, and the lender decides each deal on its numbers.
