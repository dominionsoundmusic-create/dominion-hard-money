How a hard money loan works

A hard money loan is short-term financing secured by an investment property. The lender sizes the loan on the property: what it is worth today, what it will be worth after the work, and how the loan gets repaid. Your income, your W-2 and your debt-to-income ratio matter far less than they do at a bank.

The usual structure is interest only, with the whole balance due at the end of the term. You pay interest each month while you renovate or stabilize the property, then repay the principal by selling it or refinancing into a long-term loan. Market terms commonly run 6 to 18 months.

These are business-purpose loans. The borrower is normally an LLC or corporation, and the property cannot be one you live in. For a plain-English walk through the structure and the vocabulary, read what is a hard money loan.

The four types of hard money loan

Most investor financing that gets called "hard money" falls into one of four products. Each one is built for a different exit.

Fix and flipPays for the purchase and the renovation. The renovation money is held back and released in draws as work is inspected. Repaid by selling the finished house. See fix and flip loans.
BridgeShort-term money to close now and arrange permanent financing or a sale later: an auction purchase, buying before you sell another property, or holding while a refinance is processed. No renovation holdback. See bridge loans.
DSCR rentalNot short-term at all: a 30-year loan sized on the rent the property earns rather than your personal income. It is the usual exit from a fix and hold. See DSCR rental loans.
ConstructionGround-up building on a lot you own or are buying, funded against the finished value with draws at each inspected stage. Usually reserved for builders with completed projects behind them. See construction loans.

What hard money loans cost in 2026

Across the market, short-term hard money commonly prices between about 8% and 15% interest, with 1.5 to 3 origination points paid at closing. One point is 1% of the loan amount. Add appraisal, processing, title and per-draw inspection fees on top.

Who qualifies

  • A business borrower. An LLC or corporation holds the property. If you do not have one yet, form it before you apply.
  • An investment property. Non-owner-occupied only. A home you will live in, even part of the year, is a different kind of loan.
  • A credit score of at least 600 for our lending partners' short-term programs. Above that, the deal does most of the talking.
  • A deal with room in it. The loan cannot exceed 70% of the after-repair value, so the purchase and rehab must leave enough margin to cover the rest, your costs and a profit.
  • A written exit. Sell, or refinance into a DSCR loan. The lender wants to see how the balance gets repaid before the term ends.

Requirements in more detail are on hard money requirements, and if your credit is the sticking point, read hard money with bad credit.

Choosing the right loan for the deal

Start from the exit and work backward. If the house needs work and you will sell, it is a fix and flip loan. If the house needs work and you will keep it, it is a fix and flip or rehab loan now and a DSCR refinance later, so check the rent will cover the future payment before you buy. If the house is ready but you need to close faster than a bank, it is a bridge loan. If there is no house yet, it is construction.

The common mistake is borrowing short for a long plan. A 12-month loan on a property you intend to hold for ten years only works if the refinance is lined up and the numbers support it on the day you need it.

Hard money loan questions

How do hard money loans work?

A hard money loan is a short-term, interest-only loan secured by an investment property. The lender sizes it on the property's value and your exit plan rather than your income. You pay interest monthly and repay the balance at the end of the term by selling or refinancing.

What are the different types of hard money loans?

The four main products are fix and flip (purchase plus renovation, repaid by a sale), bridge (short-term money with no renovation), DSCR rental (a 30-year loan sized on rent) and construction (ground-up building). Each has its own leverage and requirements.

How much does a hard money loan cost?

Market pricing commonly runs about 8% to 15% interest plus 1.5 to 3 points. Our lending partners' standard fix and flip and bridge pricing is 12.99% plus 2.99 points, with 10.99% plus 1.99 points available only after two loans have been paid off with them in good standing.

What credit score do I need for a hard money loan?

Our lending partners set a 600 minimum for fix and flip, rehab and bridge loans. Their DSCR rental program has no minimum credit score, though your score still affects the terms the lender offers.

Can I get a hard money loan for a house I will live in?

No. These are business-purpose loans on non-owner-occupied investment property, held in an LLC or corporation. A home you will occupy needs a consumer mortgage.

How fast can a hard money loan close?

Faster than a bank, because the lender decides on the property rather than a full income file. The appraisal, title work and your entity documents set the pace, and the lender decides the closing date. Order the appraisal and title the day you have a term sheet.
