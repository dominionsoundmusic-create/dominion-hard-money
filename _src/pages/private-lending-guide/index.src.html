Why most declined deals were never going to fund

Most deals that fall apart in underwriting were never going to fund. Not because the investor was a bad risk, but because nobody told them the rules before they signed a purchase contract.

This free 15-page guide sets the rules out in plain English, from the published terms of the lending partner we work with, and runs a real first flip through every one of them: a $180,000 purchase, $40,000 of work, and $300,000 after repair.

What is inside

The real priceThe advertised rate, the standard rate a first-time borrower actually starts at, and what a point costs in dollars.
How much they will lendLoan to cost against loan to value, and why the lower one wins.
The cash you still needWhy a loan covering "100 percent" of the project still needed about $40,000 of the borrower's own money.
The qualification checklistCredit, reserves, your company, your experience and the property, item by item, before you sign anything.
Five mistakes that kill dealsFamily living in the house, the no-money-down myth, first-time renovation limits, thin margins and no exit plan.
The documents to gatherEverything underwriting will ask for, so your file moves on the lender's clock instead of yours.
