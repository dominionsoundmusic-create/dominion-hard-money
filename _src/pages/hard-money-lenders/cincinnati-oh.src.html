One metro, two states

Cincinnati is one of the few large metros split by a state line. The Ohio side holds Cincinnati and Hamilton County, with Butler, Warren and Clermont counties around it. Across the river, Covington, Newport, Florence and the rest of Boone, Kenton and Campbell counties are just as much part of the market, but they sit in Kentucky.

Our lending partners review investment property on both sides. What changes at the river is the law, the closing costs and the tax bill, and those are what decide whether the same house makes more sense in Ohio or Kentucky.

What changes when you cross the river

Landlord law. Ohio's landlord and tenant law applies statewide: a three-day written notice to leave before an eviction filing, and a security deposit returned with an itemized list of deductions within 30 days after the tenancy ends. Interest is owed on the part of a deposit above $50 or one month's rent, whichever is greater, if the tenant stays six months or more. In Kentucky, the Uniform Residential Landlord and Tenant Act applies only in cities and counties that have adopted it; elsewhere the lease and older statutes govern. Check whether the Kentucky city where the property sits has adopted the Act before you write a lease.

Closing costs. Ohio charges a conveyance fee on each sale: a mandatory $1 per $1,000 of value plus up to $3 per $1,000 more if the county adopts it, so up to $4 per $1,000 in total, customarily paid by the seller. Kentucky's transfer tax is 50 cents per $500 of value, or $1 per $1,000. On a $250,000 resale that is up to about $1,000 on the Ohio side and about $250 on the Kentucky side.

Rental registration. Ohio law requires owners of residential rental property to file their name, address and contact information with the county auditor; on the Ohio side, that is the Hamilton County Auditor.

Property tax. Ohio taxes 35 percent of the auditor's appraised value. Kentucky assesses property at its full fair cash value, with state, county, city and school rates on top. Compare the actual bills for two similar houses before you assume one side is cheaper.

Cincinnati's older housing

Much of Cincinnati's investor activity is in its older neighborhoods: brick homes and two-families on hillsides, many built long before 1978. That brings two practical points.

The renovation. Older masonry, steep lots and hillside drainage add cost and time. Get a contractor who has worked in the neighborhood to walk the house before you set a budget. On any pre-1978 home, federal rules require lead-safe work practices from a certified renovator when painted surfaces are disturbed, and a lead disclosure to buyers and tenants.

The loan size. Our lending partners' minimum loan is $50,000, and the loan is capped at 70 percent of after-repair value. On the lowest-priced streets, a small purchase plus a small rehab may not reach the minimum, and the value cap is only as good as the renovated sales on the same street. Pull those sales before you buy the cheapest house on the block.

Can I get a hard money loan for a Northern Kentucky property near Cincinnati?

Yes, Kentucky investment property can be submitted the same way as Ohio property. Covington, Newport, Florence and the rest of Boone, Kenton and Campbell counties are part of the Cincinnati metro. The lender reviews the property, the budget and your exit, and confirms the limits for that county.

Is it cheaper to flip in Cincinnati or across the river in Kentucky?

The closing costs favor Kentucky: its transfer tax is $1 per $1,000 of value, while Ohio counties can charge a conveyance fee of up to $4 per $1,000. Property tax and landlord rules also differ, so compare actual tax bills and the local landlord law, not just the transfer cost.

Do I have to register a Cincinnati rental property?

Yes. Ohio law requires owners of residential rental property to file their contact information with the county auditor. For a Cincinnati rental, that is the Hamilton County Auditor. Do it when you close.

How fast can I evict a tenant in Ohio?

The process starts with a three-day written notice to leave the premises, then an eviction case in municipal or county court. Court timing varies, so budget for weeks, not days.

How much will a Cincinnati fix and flip loan cost?

Our lending partners' standard pricing starts at 12.99 percent plus 2.99 points, from $50,000, up to 70 percent of after-repair value and 100 percent of cost. After two loans paid off in good standing, loyalty pricing of 10.99 percent plus 1.99 points can apply. The lender sets the final terms.

Do I need lead-safe work on an older Cincinnati house?

On a home built before 1978, federal rules require lead-safe practices by an EPA-certified renovator when work disturbs painted surfaces, and you must give buyers and tenants a lead disclosure. Build both into the budget and timeline.
