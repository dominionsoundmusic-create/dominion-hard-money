Where Kansas investors work

Kansas has two advantages for an investor, affordable houses in its larger metros and some of the lowest closing taxes in the country, and one habit that trips people up: it is a non-disclosure state. This page covers how to value a deal without public sale data, what the state does and does not tax, the landlord and foreclosure rules that shape a rental, and our lending partners' terms.

The Kansas side of Kansas City is the largest market: Johnson County, Overland Park, Olathe, Shawnee, and Kansas City, Kansas, in Wyandotte County, with Lawrence about 40 minutes west. Wichita and Sedgwick County, with Derby and Andover, is the second. Topeka and Manhattan round out the eastern half of the state.

Western Kansas is a different case. Dodge City, Garden City, Liberal and Hays are hours from the nearest large metro, and private lenders commonly look harder at property where an appraiser has few recent comparable sales and a finished house has a thin pool of buyers. If your deal is west of Salina, ask the lender about the exact address before you spend money on inspections.

Kansas keeps sale prices private

This is the Kansas fact that changes how you work. Under K.S.A. 79-1437f, the sales information a buyer and seller file with the county is not an open record, and county offices cannot hand it to the public. Look up a Kansas house online and you may find its appraised value and its listing history, but not a reliable recorded sale price.

For a flipper that matters twice. Your after-repair value, which sets both your loan and your profit, depends on comparable sales you cannot easily see. And the automated value estimates investors elsewhere use as a sanity check are weaker here, because the data behind them is thinner.

The fix is people. Work with an agent who has multiple listing service access in the neighborhood and will pull closed sales for you, and expect the lender's appraiser to lean on that same data. Build your after-repair value from those closed sales, finished to the standard you plan, not from a website estimate.

What Kansas charges, and what it does not

No transfer tax, and no mortgage registration tax. Kansas charges no state tax when a deed changes hands. It also phased out its mortgage registration tax, which was 0.26 percent of the loan amount, between 2015 and 2018; since January 1, 2019 it has not been charged at all. Recording fees still apply. On a flip financed with a short-term loan, that is money that stays in the deal at both closings.

Property tax on an 11.5 percent assessment. Kansas assesses residential property at 11.5 percent of the county appraiser's market value, and commercial property at 25 percent. A rental house is still residential, so it carries the 11.5 percent ratio, multiplied by the combined mill levy of the school district, county, city and other districts where it sits. Because those levies vary widely, pull the actual tax statement for the address and recompute it on the value you expect after renovation, since the appraiser will catch up with the improvements.

Foreclosure goes through the courts, and owners can redeem. Kansas forecloses judicially. Under K.S.A. 60-2414 the owner can generally redeem the property for 12 months after the sheriff's sale, and the court sets a three-month period when less than a third of the original debt had been paid before the default. Lenders price that in, and it is one reason a buyer at a Kansas sheriff's sale should understand what a certificate of purchase does and does not give them before counting on a quick renovation.

Kansas landlord rules for a buy-and-hold plan

The Kansas Residential Landlord and Tenant Act applies statewide, so the rules below do not change from city to city.

Security deposits are capped at one month's rent for an unfurnished unit and one and a half months' for a furnished one, plus up to half a month more if pets are allowed. The deposit comes back within 14 days after the landlord works out the deductions, and in any case within 30 days after the tenancy ends and the tenant asks for it.

Unpaid rent: the act lets a landlord end the agreement if rent stays unpaid three days after written notice. Kansas also has older notice statutes for tenancies at will that set ten days once a tenancy has run three months or more, so use a Kansas notice form or attorney to pick the right one. Other serious breaches get a notice giving 14 days to fix the problem, with the tenancy ending at least 30 days after the notice if it is not fixed.

For a DSCR loan, those rules matter less than the tax line. A Wichita or Topeka rental qualifies on its rent against principal, interest, taxes and insurance, so the property tax you recomputed above is what decides whether the ratio works.

Our lending partners' terms in Kansas

Fix and flip, rehab and bridge loans: pricing starts at 12.99 percent plus 2.99 points. Borrowers who have paid off two loans with the lending partner in good standing can qualify for loyalty pricing of 10.99 percent plus 1.99 points. Loans start at $50,000, the minimum credit score is 600, and the loan can cover up to 100 percent of cost (purchase plus rehab) but no more than 70 percent of after-repair value, whichever limit binds first. DSCR rental loans: a long-term loan qualified on the rent, from $75,000 to $2 million, up to 80 percent of value on a purchase or rate-and-term refinance, with no minimum credit score.

A Wichita example: a house worth $220,000 after renovation supports up to $154,000 at 70 percent of after-repair value. If purchase and rehab together cost $165,000, the investor brings about $11,000 plus closing costs, and Kansas adds no transfer tax at the sale. Can I get a hard money loan in Kansas?

Yes. Our lending partners review non-owner-occupied investment property in Kansas, held in an LLC or corporation. Most Kansas deals are in the Kansas City suburbs, Wichita, Topeka and Lawrence. In rural western Kansas, where comparable sales are scarce, ask about the exact address early. The lender decides each deal on its numbers.

How do I find comps in Kansas if sale prices are not public?

Kansas is a non-disclosure state: under K.S.A. 79-1437f the sales data filed with the county is not an open record. Work with an agent or appraiser who has multiple listing service access and can pull closed sales of finished homes near your property.

Does Kansas have a real estate transfer tax or mortgage tax?

Neither. Kansas has no state real estate transfer tax, and its mortgage registration tax was phased out and stopped being charged on January 1, 2019. Recording fees still apply.

How much will property tax be on a Kansas rental?

Residential property, including a rental house, is assessed at 11.5 percent of market value and then multiplied by the local mill levy, which varies by district. Recompute the bill on the value you expect after renovation rather than relying on the seller's statement.

How long is the redemption period after a Kansas foreclosure?

Generally 12 months after the sheriff's sale under K.S.A. 60-2414, or three months when less than a third of the original debt had been paid before the default. A buyer at a sheriff's sale should plan around that window.

How much security deposit can a Kansas landlord charge?

One month's rent for an unfurnished unit and one and a half months' for a furnished one, plus up to half a month more for pets. It must be returned within 14 days after deductions are figured and no later than 30 days after the tenancy ends and the tenant asks for it.
