Where Orlando investors get caught

The Orlando metro, Orange, Seminole, Osceola and Lake counties, has more than two and a half million people and one of the most active investor markets in Florida. Location is rarely the problem here. What catches Orlando investors out are three things particular to Central Florida: vacation-rental plans that depend on local rules and on how a lender counts income, older condo buildings facing post-Surfside repairs, and Florida's taxes on both the deed and the loan.

Vacation rentals: check before you count on them

Orlando draws investors who want short-term rentals near the theme parks. Two cautions before you buy on that plan.

The rules are local. Whether a house can be rented by the night depends on its city or county, its zoning and often its homeowners association. Parts of Osceola County near the parks are built as resort communities where nightly rentals are expected; many other Central Florida neighborhoods restrict or prohibit them. Confirm the rules for the exact address, in writing, before you buy.

The financing is different. A DSCR loan is sized on rent, and lenders commonly qualify a property on long-term lease income. If your plan only works on nightly-rental income, do not assume a DSCR loan will count it. Ask the lender to confirm in writing for your specific property, and run the numbers on a long-term lease as the fallback.

Condos after Surfside

Orlando has a large stock of older condos, many of them bought by investors. After the 2021 Surfside collapse, Florida required milestone structural inspections for many condominium buildings three stories or taller and structural integrity reserve studies, and some associations have levied large special assessments to catch up on repairs and reserves.

That has made some condo units hard to sell and hard to finance. Before you buy a unit, ask the association for its most recent milestone inspection, its reserve study, its budget and any pending or planned special assessments. A low price on a unit in a building facing a big assessment is not a discount.

Florida's closing taxes and property tax

Documentary stamp tax on the deed: 70 cents per $100 of price, customarily paid by the seller outside Miami-Dade. When you resell a $350,000 house, that is $2,450.

Taxes on your loan: 35 cents per $100 of the note and a 0.2 percent intangible tax on the mortgage, both usually paid by the borrower. On a $250,000 hard money loan, about $875 plus $500. Put both in your closing costs.

Property tax resets when you buy. Florida limits yearly increases in the assessed value of non-homestead residential property to 10 percent, but the limit resets on a change of ownership, so the assessment moves toward market value for the new owner. Estimate a rental's tax, and a DSCR loan's payment, on the price you paid.

Insurance. Central Florida is inland, but wind coverage is still a major cost and inland flooding is real. If you insure through Citizens, its flood requirement reaches all personal residential policies with wind coverage by January 1, 2027. Get real quotes during your inspection period.

Can I get a hard money loan to flip a house in Orlando?

Yes, for non-owner-occupied investment property. Our lending partners' short-term loans start at $50,000 with a 600 minimum credit score, priced from 12.99 percent plus 2.99 points. The lender decides each file.

Can I use a DSCR loan on an Orlando vacation rental?

Be careful. DSCR lenders commonly qualify a property on long-term rental income. Do not count on nightly-rental income to qualify a loan unless the lender confirms it for your specific property in writing, and check that the address allows nightly rentals at all.

What closing taxes will I pay on an Orlando hard money loan?

Florida charges documentary stamp tax of 35 cents per $100 on the note and a 0.2 percent intangible tax on a new mortgage, which the borrower usually pays. The 70 cents per $100 deed stamp is customarily the seller's.

Will my Orlando rental's property tax go up after I buy it?

Often. Florida limits yearly increases in the assessed value of non-homestead residential property to 10 percent, but the limit resets when the property changes hands, so the new owner's assessment moves toward market value. Estimate taxes on what you paid.

Why are Orlando condos harder to finance right now?

After the 2021 Surfside collapse, Florida required milestone structural inspections and reserve studies for many condo buildings, and some associations have levied large special assessments to meet them. Ask for the association's inspection, reserve study and assessment history before you buy a unit.

How quickly can a Florida landlord act on unpaid rent?

Florida uses a three-day notice to pay or leave, counting only business days, before an eviction filing. Security deposits must be returned within 15 days, or a notice of claim sent within 30 days.
