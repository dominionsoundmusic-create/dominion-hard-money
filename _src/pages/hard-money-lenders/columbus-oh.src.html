Ohio's growth market, and a busy one

Columbus is the fastest-growing big metro in Ohio, anchored by state government, a large university, health systems and a string of large employers on its edges. For investors, that means demand from renters and buyers across a wide range of price points, from older neighborhoods near downtown to newer suburbs in Delaware and Licking counties.

It also means competition. ATTOM's second-quarter 2026 flipping report, released in October 2026, put flips at 9.5 percent of home sales in the Columbus metro, tied for second among metros of a million or more people and well above the 6.2 percent national rate. A lot of other investors are looking at the same houses you are, so the profit has to be made on the purchase.

Register the rental with the county

Ohio law requires owners of residential rental property to file their name, address and contact information with the county auditor, and counties can enforce the requirement. If you own through an LLC, the filing names who is responsible for the property. For a Columbus rental, that means the Franklin County Auditor, and it belongs on your closing checklist.

The state's landlord rules are straightforward. A three-day written notice to leave comes before an eviction filing. Security deposits have no cap, but they must be returned with an itemized list within 30 days after the tenancy ends, and interest is owed on the part above $50 or one month's rent, whichever is greater, if the tenant stays six months or more.

A DSCR loan qualifies a Columbus rental on its rent rather than your income. Reappraisals move Columbus tax bills

Ohio taxes 35 percent of the county auditor's appraised value. Counties reappraise every six years, with an update at the three-year midpoint, and in a fast-growing county a reappraisal can raise values sharply. Ohio's tax-reduction factors limit how much of a value increase turns into higher voted levies, but they do not stop every bill from rising.

For an investor, the rule is simple: use the auditor's current value for the property, and check the auditor's schedule for the next reappraisal or update, before you estimate a rental's taxes. A 2022 state law also sharply limited school boards' ability to challenge the value of property they do not own, so a purchase in Ohio is less likely to set off a value appeal than in some neighboring states.

Conveyance fee: Ohio charges a conveyance fee on each sale, a mandatory $1 per $1,000 of value plus up to $3 per $1,000 more where the county adopts it, customarily paid by the seller. On a $280,000 resale, that is at most about $1,120.

Is Columbus a good market for flipping houses in 2026?

It is a busy one. ATTOM put flips at 9.5 percent of Columbus-area home sales in the second quarter of 2026, tied for second among large metros. Strong demand helps the exit, but heavy competition means the deal has to be bought right.

Do I have to register my Columbus rental property?

Yes. Ohio law requires owners of residential rental property to file their contact information with the county auditor, and counties can enforce it. If the owner is an LLC, the filing names who is responsible. Register a Columbus rental with the Franklin County Auditor when you buy it.

Will my Columbus property taxes go up after a reappraisal?

They can. Ohio taxes 35 percent of the auditor's appraised value, and counties reappraise every six years with an update in between. Tax-reduction factors soften the effect on voted levies, but check the current auditor value and the reappraisal schedule before you estimate a rental's taxes.

What notice does an Ohio landlord give before an eviction?
