Cleveland is the busiest flipping market among big metros

ATTOM's report on the first quarter of 2026 found that flips made up 12.1 percent of all home sales in the Cleveland metro, second only to Atlanta among metros of a million people or more. In the second quarter, released in October 2026, Cleveland moved to first among large metros at 10.4 percent, against 6.2 percent nationally. Nearby Canton (11.6 percent) and Akron (11.2 percent) were among the five most-flipped metros of any size.

Low prices and old, solid housing are the draw. The flip side of that much investor activity is that you are bidding against many other flippers for the same houses, and the profit is made on the purchase price.

How a Cleveland flip gets sized

Our lending partners lend up to 70 percent of after-repair value and up to 100 percent of cost (purchase plus rehab), whichever limit is reached first, with a minimum loan of $50,000.

Take a house bought for $110,000 with $70,000 of work and an after-repair value of $260,000. Seventy percent of $260,000 is $182,000, enough on paper to cover the whole $180,000 cost. Now cut the ARV to $220,000 because the comps were thinner than you hoped: 70 percent is $154,000, and you bring $26,000 of the cost yourself, plus points and closing costs. In Cleveland, the after-repair value decides how much cash you need far more than the rate does.

How much of those limits you get depends on the experience you can document over the past three years, and limits can vary by county and property. Confirm them with the lender for your specific address, inside Cuyahoga County or out in Lake, Lorain, Medina, Summit or Geauga, before you commit.

The $50,000 minimum also matters here. On Cleveland's lowest-priced streets, a small purchase plus a small rehab may not reach it, and the renovated sales on the same street may not support the value you need.

Cleveland's lead-safe rule for rentals

Most of Cleveland's housing was built before 1978, and the city has made lead a condition of renting. Under Cleveland's lead-safe ordinance, rental units built before 1978 must hold a Lead Safe Certificate, based on a lead inspection by a state-approved lead professional, and rentals must also be registered with the city.

For an investor who plans to hold, that belongs in the renovation plan: the lead-safe work, the inspection and the registration before the first tenant moves in. For a flipper whose buyer is a landlord, a house that already has its certificate is worth more to that buyer. Either way, any renovation crew working on a pre-1978 house must follow federal lead-safe work practices.

Ohio's landlord and tax rules

Evictions: a three-day written notice to leave, then a case in municipal or county court.

Security deposits: no cap, but the deposit and an itemized list of deductions must go back within 30 days after the tenancy ends, and interest is owed on the part of a deposit above $50 or one month's rent, whichever is greater, if the tenant stays six months or more.

Rental registration with the county: on top of the city's rules, Ohio law requires owners of residential rental property to file their contact information with the county auditor.

Property tax: Ohio taxes 35 percent of a property's appraised value, and counties reappraise on a set cycle. A 2022 state law sharply limited school boards' ability to file value challenges on property they do not own, which makes a purchase less likely to set off an appeal than in some neighboring states. Estimate taxes on the county auditor's current value, not an old listing figure.

Conveyance fee: Ohio charges a conveyance fee on each sale, a mandatory $1 per $1,000 of value plus up to $3 per $1,000 more where the county adopts it, customarily paid by the seller.

How competitive is flipping in Cleveland right now?

Very. ATTOM put flips at 12.1 percent of Cleveland-area home sales in the first quarter of 2026 and 10.4 percent in the second, the highest rate of any metro with a million or more people that quarter. You are competing with many other investors for the same houses.

How much cash do I need for a Cleveland fix and flip loan?

It depends on the after-repair value. Our lending partners lend up to 70 percent of ARV and 100 percent of cost, whichever is lower, so a deal whose costs run above 70 percent of a realistic ARV needs your cash for the gap, plus points, closing costs and reserves.

Do Cleveland rentals need a Lead Safe Certificate?

If the building was put up before 1978, yes. Cleveland requires a Lead Safe Certificate, based on a lead inspection by a state-approved lead professional, and rental registration with the city. Plan the inspection and any lead work into your renovation.

Is there a minimum loan size for a cheap Cleveland house?

Yes. Our lending partners' minimum loan is $50,000. On some of Cleveland's lowest-priced streets a small purchase and rehab will not reach it, so check the numbers before you go under contract.

How much security deposit can a Cleveland landlord charge?

Ohio does not cap the amount. A landlord must return the deposit, with an itemized list of deductions, within 30 days after the tenancy ends, and owes interest on the part above $50 or one month's rent, whichever is greater, if the tenant stays six months or more.
