Where San Antonio deals come from right now

San Antonio is one of the most affordable big metros in Texas to start investing in, with steady rental demand and suburbs that keep growing: New Braunfels, Schertz, Converse, Boerne, Helotes and Seguin. Entry prices are lower than in Austin or Dallas, which is why many first-time Texas investors start here.

But the flip math is the thinnest in the country. ATTOM's report on the second quarter of 2026 put the typical gross flipping margin in the San Antonio metro at a loss of 0.3 percent: the typical flipped house resold for slightly less than the flipper paid, before a dollar of renovation, points, interest, insurance, taxes or commissions. Dallas and Austin were barely positive at 1.8 and 2.8 percent, against 21.5 percent nationally.

That points San Antonio investors in two directions. Flips that work are bought far below the typical discount, often houses that need more work than retail buyers or other flippers will take on. And many investors here make their money on rentals instead.

San Antonio as a rental market

San Antonio's rental demand is broad: a large and steady workforce, several universities, and military households tied to Joint Base San Antonio, which includes Lackland, Randolph and Fort Sam Houston. Military renters move on a schedule, so a rental near the bases can turn over often, but it also tends to re-rent.

A DSCR loan suits this market because it qualifies the property on its rent, not on your tax return. Our lending partners' DSCR program lends from $75,000 to $2 million, at up to 80 percent of value on a purchase and no minimum credit score. The appraiser estimates market rent, and the full payment, including property tax and insurance, has to be covered by that rent.

That is where two San Antonio costs come in. Property tax: a rental the seller has held for years may be taxed well below market until you buy it, because Bexar County will reappraise on the sale. Texas currently caps yearly increases in the appraised value of most non-homestead real property at 20 percent, but only from the year after you acquire it, and that cap is set to expire on December 31, 2026 unless the Legislature extends it. And flood insurance: parts of the city sit in mapped flood hazard areas along the creeks and the river, where the lender will require it. Check both during your option period.

Comps in a non-disclosure state

Texas is a non-disclosure state, so sale prices are not public record. Website estimates in San Antonio are built on thin data, and with typical resale margins this tight, a valuation that is a few percent high wipes out the deal. Pull closed sales of renovated homes on the same street from the multiple listing service through a local agent, and build your after-repair value from those.

The cheapest purchase in a neighborhood is not always a deal. On parts of the South and West Sides, finished values sit close to purchase prices plus rehab, which leaves no room for the cost of the money. Run the numbers on the finished value first, then work back to the offer.

Texas rules that help San Antonio investors

No transfer tax on the sale, and no state income tax on rental profits.

Fast foreclosure. Texas generally forecloses outside court, with sales on the first Tuesday of the month at the county. Foreclosure auctions are a real source of discounted San Antonio deals, but they settle quickly in certified funds, so the financing has to be arranged before the sale, not after.

A protest window. If the Bexar Appraisal District values your purchase above what you paid, the usual protest deadline is May 15, or 30 days after your notice is delivered, whichever is later.

Is flipping houses in San Antonio still profitable?

The typical flip was not in mid 2026. ATTOM put the typical gross flipping margin in the San Antonio metro at a loss of 0.3 percent in the second quarter, before renovation, financing and selling costs. Flips that work are bought at a much deeper discount than the typical one.

Should I flip or rent in San Antonio?

On 2026 numbers, rentals are often the easier case. Entry prices are lower than in Austin or Dallas and rental demand is broad, including from military households around Joint Base San Antonio. A DSCR loan qualifies a rental on its rent rather than your income.

Will a San Antonio rental's property tax go up after I buy it?

Often, yes. The sale gives the appraisal district a reason to revalue, and the 20 percent yearly cap on non-homestead property applies only from the year after you acquire it and is set to expire after 2026 unless extended. Estimate your taxes on market value, not the seller's bill.

When is the Bexar County property tax protest deadline?

Usually May 15, or 30 days after your appraisal notice is delivered, whichever is later.

Do I need flood insurance in San Antonio?

In a mapped flood hazard area, a lender will require it. Check the property's flood zone and get an insurance quote during your option period, because it can change a rental's numbers.
