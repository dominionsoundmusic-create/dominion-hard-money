The thing that made Colorado work has stopped

For most of the last decade, Front Range deals penciled because prices climbed. Thin cash flow did not matter much when the property gained value faster than it cost to hold. That arithmetic has changed: prices along the Front Range have flattened, more homes are sitting on the market, and rents have stopped rising the way they did.

So on the Front Range, buy-and-hold at full market price often runs at or near break-even on cash flow. That is not a crisis. It is a different game: the market now favors buyers who negotiate and add value rather than buyers who wait for the market to bail them out.

Two things follow, and they run through everything below. The yield is south, not north. And on the Front Range the money is in value-add rather than in buying at market and holding: Denver's 1980s and 1990s multifamily and single-family stock is where that work sits.

Drive south on I-25 and the yield climbs

One highway, about a hundred miles, and three different investments. This is the clearest structural fact in Colorado, and a statewide average hides it.

The gap is driven by the price base more than by rents. Pueblo rents are lower than Colorado Springs rents in dollars, but the entry price falls faster than the rent does, so the yield rises.

The trade for that yield is liquidity. Pueblo homes generally take longer to sell than Denver homes. On a rental that barely matters. On a flip it can be most of your term, and it is the most common way a Pueblo deal goes wrong: an investor buys the yield and models a Denver exit. Pull days on market from closed sales in the exact neighborhood and budget the carry for it.

The markets, and what each one is for

Colorado Springs: the balance point

Fort Carson, Peterson and Schriever Space Force Base and the Air Force Academy give the city a steady military and defense-contractor tenant base. Submarket choice matters more than the city average: Fountain, near Fort Carson, trades on yield and a military tenant base, while Briargate on the north side trades on school quality and appreciation, with lower yields. Northgate picks up Denver commuters off I-25, which widens the tenant pool beyond the military.

Pueblo: the yield end

Low entry prices in established neighborhoods are why investors look here. The honest risk is economic concentration: Pueblo has fewer large employers than the northern Front Range, so underwrite the yield, then ask what happens to it if the biggest employer in town has a bad year.

Denver metro: value-add or nothing

At Denver prices, buying at market and holding rarely clears a lender's coverage test. What does work is older stock where rents can be moved by improving the asset. Aurora, Commerce City and Brighton offer lower entry prices than the core city: better, still thin, and dependent on buying below market. Denver also requires a residential rental license, with an inspection, before a property can be rented, so put it on the renovation timeline.

The northern Front Range

Fort Collins, Loveland, Longmont and Greeley run on university, tech and agricultural employment. Boulder is a high-barrier appreciation market and should be underwritten as one, not as a cash-flow rental.

The Western Slope

Grand Junction is the largest market west of the Divide, with small multifamily that most out-of-state investors never look at because they never look past the Front Range.

Colorado property tax, transfer costs and foreclosure

Property tax. Colorado assesses residential property, including a long-term rental house, at a much lower percentage of value than commercial property, and the legislature has changed the residential rate almost every year since 2022. Counties reappraise every odd-numbered year, using sales from a set study period. Two consequences for an investor: a reappraisal year can move the tax bill sharply, and a purchase price well above the last appraisal will catch up at the next one. Use the county assessor's current rate and the expected value, not the seller's old bill.

Transfer cost. Colorado has no real estate transfer tax in the usual sense. The state charges a documentary fee of one cent per $100 of the price, so a $400,000 sale costs $40. A few mountain towns add their own real estate transfer tax, so check the municipality before you budget a resort-area flip.

Foreclosure. Most Colorado foreclosures run through the county public trustee rather than a full lawsuit, with a short court hearing under Rule 120 of the state's civil rules to authorize the sale. The owner can cure the default before the sale. Colorado removed the owner's right to redeem after the sale, though some junior lienholders still have one, so a public trustee purchase can reach insurable title faster than in states with a long redemption clock. Ask a title company what it will insure before you bid.

Colorado landlord rules that change a rental's numbers

Colorado's landlord law has moved toward tenants in the last few years, and each change shows up in a buy-and-hold underwriting.

For-cause eviction. Since 2024, most residential landlords need a stated cause, such as nonpayment or a lease violation, to remove a tenant who has been in place for 12 months, with limited exceptions. A buy-and-hold plan that counts on turning a tenant over to raise rent is harder to execute.

Nonpayment. A landlord must give a written 10-day demand for rent before filing an eviction for nonpayment, which lengthens the time a unit can sit without income.

Security deposits. Colorado caps deposits at two months' rent and requires their return within one month after the lease ends, or up to 60 days if the lease says so.

Local licensing. Denver requires a rental license and inspection, and other cities have their own registration and short-term rental rules. Mountain towns from Breckenridge to Steamboat limit short-term rentals tightly.

A DSCR loan is sized on the rent the property can carry. Budget the longer vacancy these rules create, and if the plan is a short-term rental, test the deal on long-term rent too, since a rule change can take the nightly revenue away.

Insurance here is a hail question first

Colorado gets described as a wildfire insurance market, but along the Front Range the larger cost driver is hail. Data the state's Division of Insurance collected from 20 carriers and published in February 2026 put hail at about half of the average homeowners premium along the Front Range and Eastern Plains, against roughly 1 percent for wildfire in Denver, and Colorado regularly ranks among the states with the most hail damage claims.

Wildfire still matters, but differently. It is an availability problem more than a price one: a house in a high-risk area of the wildland-urban interface can be declined by standard carriers altogether rather than merely quoted high. So the question on a given address is not only what the premium is, it is whether standard coverage can be bound at all.

Either way it is a due-diligence item rather than a budget guess. Get a bindable quote on the specific address during diligence, before you are committed, and ask what an impact-resistant roof would take off it. The premium sits inside the DSCR payment, so on a thin Front Range deal a premium taken from a national average rather than a quote can move the coverage ratio below the level you were counting on.

Can I get a hard money loan anywhere in Colorado?

Our lending partners review deals across Colorado: the Front Range from Fort Collins to Pueblo, the Western Slope and the mountain towns. Each deal is sized on its after-repair value, cost and your experience, and the lender decides.

Should I buy rentals in Colorado Springs or Pueblo?

Colorado Springs if you want a middle yield with a more liquid exit and a military tenant base. Pueblo if cash flow is the objective and you can carry a slower sale. The difference in entry price is the whole trade.

Is Denver still worth buying for an investor?

For value-add, yes: older housing where rents can be moved by improving the property is where the work is, and more inventory means negotiating room. For buying at market and holding, the cash flow rarely works.

Does Colorado have a transfer tax on a flip?

Not a large one. The state documentary fee is one cent per $100 of the price. Some mountain towns charge their own real estate transfer tax, so check the municipality.

Can a former owner redeem a Colorado house after a public trustee sale?

Colorado removed the owner's right to redeem after the sale, though certain junior lienholders can still redeem. Have a title company confirm what it will insure before you bid.

How do Colorado's new landlord laws affect a DSCR rental?

For-cause eviction rules, a 10-day demand before a nonpayment eviction and a two-month deposit cap all lengthen the time a unit can sit without income. Budget vacancy for them, and use a real insurance quote, since the premium sits inside the DSCR payment.
