Southern New Hampshire and the Seacoast

Southern New Hampshire is one of New England's steadier investor markets, close to Boston and well supplied with buyers and renters. Two state rules change the math: a transfer tax charged to both buyer and seller, and a good cause requirement for ending most tenancies. This page covers both, plus property tax and foreclosure, then our lending partners' terms.

Manchester and Nashua, with Bedford, Merrimack, Hudson, Londonderry and Derry, form the state's largest market. The Seacoast and the Boston-facing towns, Portsmouth, Dover, Rochester, Exeter, Salem and Windham, draw on the Boston metro. Concord sits about half an hour north of Manchester.

Where it gets harder: the Lakes Region around Laconia, Keene, the Upper Valley around Lebanon and Hanover, and the North Country beyond the White Mountains have smaller markets and more seasonal and rural property. Private lenders commonly look harder where comparable sales are scarce, so ask about an address north of Concord before you commit.

New Hampshire's two-sided transfer tax

New Hampshire has no general sales tax and no tax on wages, but it does tax real estate transfers, and it charges both sides. Under RSA 78-B the rate is 75 cents per $100 of the price for the buyer and the same for the seller, 1.5 percent in total.

That matters on a flip, because you are the buyer at the start and the seller at the end. Buy for $300,000 and sell for $420,000, and your share is about $2,250 on the purchase and $3,150 on the sale: roughly $5,400 in transfer tax on one project. Put it in your numbers alongside commissions from the first day.

Property taxes are the other large cost. With no broad sales or income tax, New Hampshire relies heavily on property tax, and rates vary widely from town to town. Get the town's current rate and the latest bill for the exact address before you estimate a rental's holding costs or size a DSCR loan.

New Hampshire's landlord rules include good cause

This is the New Hampshire rule most out-of-state investors do not expect. RSA 540:1-a divides rental property into restricted and nonrestricted property. For restricted property, which covers most residential rentals, a landlord may end a tenancy only for a reason the statute lists in RSA 540:2: nonpayment, substantial damage, a lease violation, behavior that threatens health or safety, or other good cause, which can include a legitimate business or economic reason. Certain small rentals, such as some single-family houses owned by a landlord with only a few, are nonrestricted. Check which category your property falls into before you plan to reposition a building.

Unpaid rent: a seven-day eviction notice after a demand for rent. The tenant can stop the eviction by paying everything owed plus a $15 fee, but only three times in 12 months (RSA 540:9).

Other lease violations and good cause: generally a 30-day notice that states the reason.

Security deposits: no more than one month's rent or $100, whichever is greater, returned within 30 days after the tenancy ends with an itemized list of deductions (RSA 540-A).

Foreclosure and our lending partners' terms

Foreclosure by power of sale. New Hampshire lenders generally foreclose outside court under the statutory power of sale in RSA 479:25, after published and mailed notice. A borrower who wants to stop the sale must go to court before it happens, and there is no redemption period after the sale.

Can I get a hard money loan in New Hampshire?

Yes. Our lending partners review non-owner-occupied investment property in New Hampshire held in an LLC or corporation, most often in Manchester, Nashua, Concord and the Seacoast. Farther north, ask about the exact address early. The lender decides each deal.

How much transfer tax does a flip pay in New Hampshire?

The tax is 75 cents per $100 for the buyer and the same for the seller. On a flip you pay the buyer's share when you buy and the seller's share when you sell, about $5,400 on a $300,000 purchase and $420,000 resale.

Does New Hampshire require good cause to evict?

For restricted property, which covers most residential rentals, yes. A landlord may end the tenancy only for a reason listed in RSA 540:2, such as nonpayment, damage, a lease violation or other good cause.

How does a New Hampshire landlord handle unpaid rent?

With a seven-day eviction notice after a demand for rent. The tenant can stop the eviction by paying everything owed plus a $15 fee, but only three times in a 12-month period.

How much security deposit can a New Hampshire landlord charge?

No more than one month's rent or $100, whichever is greater. It must be returned within 30 days after the tenancy ends, with an itemized list of any deductions.

Is there a redemption period after a New Hampshire foreclosure?

No. Most New Hampshire foreclosures use the statutory power of sale, and once the sale is held the former owner cannot redeem.
