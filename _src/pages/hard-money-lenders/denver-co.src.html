The question in Denver is cost, not financing

The city and the counties around it, Arapahoe, Jefferson, Adams, Douglas and Broomfield, sit inside a metro of about three million people, and lenders are comfortable lending in Colorado. So the question in Denver is rarely whether a deal can be financed. It is whether the costs that are specific to Colorado leave enough profit.

Time is one of them. In a special analysis of first-quarter 2026 loan-level flips in six markets, ATTOM and Backflip found Denver projects took an average of about 133 days to pay off, second-longest after Austin and well behind Atlanta's roughly 90. Budget the holding costs on that kind of timeline.

Hail is the cost to price first

In most of the country, a rental's biggest surprise is taxes. In Denver it is insurance, and the reason is hail. A February 2026 analysis by Colorado's Division of Insurance, based on data from 20 insurers writing about 80 percent of the state's homeowners market, found insurers attributed about 51 percent of the average Denver homeowners premium to hail, roughly $1,550 of an estimated $3,040, and about 1 percent to wildfire.

For an investor that means two things. Get an actual insurance quote during your inspection period, not an estimate, because it can change whether a rental covers its payment on a DSCR loan. And if the renovation includes a roof, ask the carrier to quote it both ways: many Colorado insurers offer discounts for Class 3 or Class 4 impact-resistant roofing.

Renting in Denver: license and cause

A rental license. The City and County of Denver requires a residential rental license for long-term rentals: buildings with two or more units since January 1, 2023, and single-unit rentals since January 1, 2024. Getting the license takes an inspection by a certified inspector, licenses are renewed with a new inspection every four years, and a license does not pass to a buyer, so a new owner needs a fresh inspection and license before renting.

A reason to end a tenancy. Colorado's 2024 for-cause eviction law (HB24-1098) requires landlords to have a listed reason to evict or decline to renew most tenants who have lived in the unit for 12 months or more. No-fault reasons are limited and come with longer notice. That changes the plan for an investor who expects to buy an occupied rental, renovate and re-tenant at a higher rent. Check the current rules and your situation with a Colorado attorney first.

Unpaid rent. Colorado requires a ten-day written demand before an eviction filing, longer than many states.

Colorado's smaller costs

No real transfer tax in Denver. Colorado charges a state documentary fee of one cent per $100 of value, $30 on a $300,000 sale. Some resort towns add their own real estate transfer taxes, but the City and County of Denver does not.

Foreclosure through the public trustee. Colorado forecloses through each county's public trustee rather than a full court case, which keeps lenders comfortable in the state.

Do I need a rental license for a Denver investment property?

Yes. Denver requires a residential rental license for long-term rentals, including single-unit homes since January 2024. You need an inspection by a certified inspector to get it, it renews every four years, and a license does not transfer when you buy, so plan a new inspection after closing.

Why is homeowners insurance so expensive on a Denver rental?

Hail. The Colorado Division of Insurance's February 2026 analysis found insurers attributed about 51 percent of the average Denver premium to hail. Get a real quote during your inspection period and ask about discounts for impact-resistant roofing.

Can I evict a tenant in Colorado to renovate and re-rent?

Often not without cause. Colorado's 2024 for-cause law requires a listed reason to evict or decline to renew most tenants who have lived in the unit for 12 months or more, and no-fault reasons are limited. Talk to a Colorado attorney before you buy an occupied rental with that plan.

How long does a Denver flip usually take?

In ATTOM and Backflip's first-quarter 2026 analysis, Denver flips averaged about 133 days to payoff. Budget interest, insurance and taxes for at least that long, and longer if permits or a roof replacement are involved.

What does a Denver fix and flip loan cost?

Our lending partners' standard pricing starts at 12.99 percent plus 2.99 points, from $50,000, up to 70 percent of after-repair value and 100 percent of cost. After two loans paid off in good standing, loyalty pricing of 10.99 percent plus 1.99 points can apply. The lender sets the final terms.

Is there a transfer tax when I sell a house in Denver?

Only Colorado's small state documentary fee, one cent per $100 of value, when the deed is recorded. Some mountain towns add their own real estate transfer taxes, but the City and County of Denver does not.
