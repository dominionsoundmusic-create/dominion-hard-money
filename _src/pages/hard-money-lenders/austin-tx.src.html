The thinnest flip margins in the country

Location is rarely the question in Austin. Central Austin, Round Rock, Georgetown, Cedar Park, Pflugerville, Kyle, Buda and San Marcos all sit inside a metro of more than two million people. The flip numbers are the question.

ATTOM's report on the first quarter of 2026 put the typical gross flipping margin in the Austin metro at 2 percent, the lowest of any metro of a million people or more. Its second-quarter report, released in October 2026, had Austin at 2.8 percent, still among the five thinnest large metros alongside San Antonio, Dallas and Houston. On a $450,000 purchase, a 2.8 percent spread is a resale around $463,000, before renovation, financing, taxes, insurance and commissions, which ATTOM notes flippers commonly estimate at 20 to 33 percent of the resale price. At the typical spread, the typical Austin flip loses money.

Austin flips also take time. In a special analysis of first-quarter 2026 loan-level deals across six markets, ATTOM and Backflip found Austin had the longest average payoff, about 154 days, against roughly 90 days in Atlanta. Five months of interest, taxes and insurance is real money on a thin spread.

That does not mean Austin investors have no plays. It means the plays are different: buying well below market, adding value a cosmetic flip cannot, or building.

Austin's HOME changes are the opportunity to study

The City of Austin's HOME initiative was adopted in two phases. Phase 1, approved in December 2023, allows up to three homes on many lots that once held one. Phase 2, approved in May 2024, cut the minimum lot size for a single-family house to 1,800 square feet in many single-family zones.

For an investor, that turns some Austin properties from a thin flip into a different project: split a lot and build new houses, add units behind an existing home, or buy a teardown for the land. Before you plan around it, confirm the current rules, your lot's zoning, deed restrictions and utility capacity with the city and a local land-use professional. Many neighborhoods carry private deed restrictions that the city rules do not override.

Rentals in Austin: check the tax and the rent

Austin rents have softened with the wave of new apartments, so run a rental on today's rent, not last year's. A DSCR loan qualifies the property on that rent rather than your income. Our lending partners' 30-year fixed DSCR program has no minimum credit score and lends from $50,000 to $2 million per property, up to 80 percent of value on a purchase or rate-and-term refinance and up to 75 percent on a cash-out refinance.

The swing number is property tax. Texas has no state income tax and makes up for it at the county and school district level. Texas also limits yearly appraisal increases on non-homestead property worth up to about $5.32 million in 2026 to 20 percent, but only after a full calendar year of ownership, the limit is removed when the property sells, and it is scheduled to expire after the 2026 tax year unless the Legislature renews it. A house the seller held for years may be taxed well below the price you pay. Estimate taxes on that price, and if the appraisal district overshoots, the usual protest deadline is May 15 or 30 days after your notice is delivered, whichever is later.

Texas rules that apply in Austin

No transfer tax. Texas does not tax the sale of real estate.

Non-disclosure. Sale prices are not public record, so after-repair values come from closed sales in the multiple listing service. In a market where the typical margin is a few percent, a value built from a website estimate is a gamble.

Fast foreclosure. Texas generally forecloses outside court under a deed of trust, with sales on the first Tuesday of the month, which keeps lenders comfortable and puts auction properties on the market regularly.

Is flipping houses in Austin still profitable in 2026?

Rarely at the typical margin. ATTOM put the typical gross flipping margin in the Austin metro at 2 percent in the first quarter of 2026 and 2.8 percent in the second, before renovation, financing and selling costs. Austin flips that work are usually bought well below market or add real value.

How long does a typical Austin flip take to pay off?

In ATTOM and Backflip's first-quarter 2026 analysis of six markets, Austin had the longest average payoff, about 154 days. Budget your interest, taxes and insurance on a five to six month hold.

Can I build more than one house on my Austin lot under HOME?

Possibly. HOME Phase 1 allows up to three homes on many lots that once held one, and Phase 2 lowered the minimum single-family lot size to 1,800 square feet in many zones. Deed restrictions, zoning and utilities still decide, so confirm with the city before you plan around it.
