What to have in front of you

The form takes a few minutes. The more you fill in, the faster the lending partner's team can tell you whether the deal fits one of its programs.

The propertyAddress or a description, the state, and what you are paying or what it is worth today.
The planYour rehab budget and the value after repair, if there is work to do.
The exitSell after the rehab, refinance into a long-term loan, or hold as a rental.
The entityWhether you have an LLC or corporation, or are setting one up.

Submitting the form is not a loan application and is not a commitment to lend. Dominion Hard Money does not lend its own funds; the lending partner reviews, decides and funds.

What happens next

  1. We review your inquiryWe check it against the lending partner's programs.
  2. We reach outWe discuss the deal with you and what the lender will want to see.
  3. You send documentsThe lender's team tells you exactly what the file needs.
  4. The lender decidesIf approved, the lending partner sets the terms and funds at closing.

Programs and starting terms

  • Fix and flip and bridge: from 12.99% interest plus 2.99 points, up to 100% of cost and 70% of value, from $50,000, minimum credit score 600. Fix and flip guide.
  • DSCR rental: qualified on the property's rent, no minimum credit score, up to 80% loan to value. DSCR guide.
  • No upfront application fees. Fees are paid at closing, and only if the loan closes.

Is this form a loan application?

No. It is an inquiry. It lets the lending partner's team see whether your deal fits a program before anyone asks you for a full application or documents.

Will submitting the form affect my credit?

The form itself does not pull your credit. If you go on to apply, ask the lender's team how and when it checks credit for your file.

Can I submit a deal for a house I will live in?

No. Every program is for non-owner-occupied investment property held in an LLC or corporation.

What if I do not have an LLC yet?

Say so on the form. Many investors set one up during the process, but the loan closes in the entity's name, so it needs to be in place before closing.
