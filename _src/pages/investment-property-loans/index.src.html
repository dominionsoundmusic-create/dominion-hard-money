Four ways to finance a rental, and they are not interchangeable

Most investors start with a conventional investment mortgage because it is the cheapest money available, and most investors eventually stop being able to use one. Knowing which of the four fits your situation, and roughly when you will outgrow it, saves months of applying for the wrong thing. The walls almost every investor hits

This is the part nobody warns you about at property one.

Your tax returns stop telling the whole story. Fannie Mae's rules have a lender add depreciation, interest, taxes, insurance and association dues back to the Schedule E figure, so a paper loss from depreciation alone is not the problem many investors fear. What does hurt is a genuine cash loss, a property bought this year with no rental history on the return, or a business return that has been written down hard. The more of your income runs through those, the harder conventional qualifying gets.

Debt-to-income tightens with every property. Each financed rental's payment enters the calculation, offset only by the rent the rules allow you to count, which is 75 percent of gross rent when it comes from a lease or the appraiser's estimate. Add properties and the ratio creeps toward the limit even when the rents plainly cover the loans.

And then there is a hard stop. Fannie Mae caps an investment property borrower at ten financed properties, counting a mortgaged home. It is a rule rather than a preference: you cannot finance an eleventh conventionally with perfect credit and a seven-figure income.

The usual path is to start conventional, hit one of those walls, and move to DSCR, which qualifies on the property's rent instead of your return and does not count how many you already own. Knowing that in advance means you can plan the transition rather than discover it mid-contract. The investment property loan requirements page walks through the conventional rules in detail.

Worth doing the arithmetic on 15% versus 25%. Below 20% down a conventional loan carries mortgage insurance, and Fannie Mae's investment property price adjustment steps up sharply above 75% loan to value: from 2.125% of the loan at 75% to 3.375% at 80% and 4.125% above that. For a borrower with a 760 score buying a $300,000 rental, the combined adjustments run about $5,340 at 25% down against about $12,110 at 15% down, before mortgage insurance. If you are holding for years, the larger down payment usually earns its keep. The full table and worked numbers are here.

Reserves matter as much as the down payment and get overlooked more often. On a conventional loan, Fannie Mae requires reserves for the property you are buying plus a percentage of what you owe on your other financed properties, and that percentage climbs as you grow. DSCR and hard money lenders set their own reserve rules, and a file with a cash cushion closes where a stronger ratio with none can stall.

Using equity you already have

The most common way investors fund a down payment without draining savings is to borrow against a property they already own: a cash-out refinance or a home equity line on a primary residence or an existing rental.

There is a specific advantage to this beyond convenience. Fannie Mae accepts documented borrowed funds secured by an asset you own toward an investment property purchase, while it does not accept gift money for one. It is how a great many portfolios get past the second or third property.

The caution is real, though. That equity line or refinance adds a payment to your debt-to-income, so it brings the conventional wall closer even as it funds the next purchase. If the plan is to keep buying, map which properties you will still be able to finance conventionally afterwards before you draw on the equity, not after.

What lenders are actually pricing

On a conventional loan, the price of an investment property is built from published adjustments layered on top of the market rate. Within that, five things move your number.

  • Credit score. 620 is Fannie Mae's general minimum, and the price adjustment falls at each higher score band. The gap between the bottom and the top bands is worth more than most people assume.
  • Leverage. Lower loan to value, lower adjustments. This is the lever you control most directly, and the 75% line matters most.
  • Reserves. Cash after closing reduces the lender's risk, and on conventional loans the required amount grows with every property you finance.
  • Property type. Two-to-four unit buildings carry an extra price adjustment on top of the investment property charge.
  • How many quotes you got. Lenders fold the same published adjustments into their rates differently. Comparing quotes at the same down payment is the fastest way to find the difference.

How much do I need to put down on an investment property?

On a conventional Fannie Mae purchase, at least 15% for a single-family rental and 25% for two to four units, and putting 25% down on a single-family avoids mortgage insurance and cuts the price adjustments sharply. Our lending partners' DSCR loans go up to 80% of value, so from 20% down.

What credit score do I need for an investment property loan?

Fannie Mae's general minimum is 620, with better pricing at each higher band. Our lending partners require 600 on fix and flip and bridge loans and set no minimum score on their 30-year DSCR rental loans.

How many investment properties can I finance with a conventional loan?

Fannie Mae allows up to ten financed properties, counting your home if it has a mortgage. Past that you need DSCR, portfolio or private financing regardless of your credit or income.

Can I use a HELOC on my house for the down payment on a rental?

Yes. Fannie Mae accepts documented funds borrowed against an asset you own, while it does not allow gift money on an investment property. Bear in mind the new payment adds to your debt-to-income and brings the conventional limit closer.

Why are investment property rates higher than a normal mortgage?

Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac charge an extra loan-level price adjustment on investment property, from 1.125% of the loan at low leverage up to 4.125% above 80% loan to value, and lenders usually fold it into the rate. The full breakdown is on our rates page.

Should I use a conventional loan or a DSCR loan for my rental?

If your income documents cleanly, you have fewer than ten financed properties and time for a full underwrite, conventional is usually cheaper. If you are self-employed, writing off heavily, buying through an entity, or scaling past the cap, DSCR is usually what keeps you moving.
