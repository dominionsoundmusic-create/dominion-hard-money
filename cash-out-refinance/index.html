Cash-Out Refinance on Investment Property

Turning trapped equity into the next deposit, without selling. What the ceiling actually is, how long you have to wait, and the exception that removes the wait entirely for cash buyers.

Three tests, all at once

A cash-out refinance on a rental has to clear the leverage cap, the credit floor and the coverage floor simultaneously. Clearing two and missing one is a decline, and a bigger appraisal does not fix it.

TestWhere it usually sitsDetail
Leverage75% LTVCash-out caps several points below what the same lender allows on a purchase. Purchase-money DSCR runs 75 to 80%, occasionally 85% around a 700 score. Cash-out does not get that ceiling. Fannie Mae caps investment cash-out at 75% too.
Coverage1.00 DSCR minRent must cover the new, larger payment. Pulling more cash raises PITIA, which lowers your ratio: the two work against each other.
CreditDrives pricing hard740 at 70% LTV prices materially better than 660 at 75% on the identical property. Waiting a few months to raise a score often saves more than it costs.

Seasoning: when you can ask

Seasoning is how long you must be on title before a lender will size the refinance off current value rather than what you paid.

  • DSCR and non-QM: most files clear at around six months of ownership. Some programs take a view at three.
  • Conventional: Fannie Mae generally wants the existing first mortgage to be at least twelve months old.
  • Important distinction: seasoning decides when you may apply. It does not decide how much gets sized off the improved value: that is a separate underwriting judgement about your appraisal and your paperwork.

Non-QM seasoning rules move more often than agency rules and vary between investors. Nobody should quote you one as a published standard. Price your actual scenario.

Delayed financing: the cash buyer's exception

If you bought for cash with no mortgage on the purchase, delayed financing lets you pull that capital back out without waiting through seasoning. It is the single most useful exception in this area and the most commonly misdescribed.

The conditions are strictArm's length purchase. A settlement statement showing no mortgage financing was used. Clean title with no existing liens. The source of your purchase funds fully documented.
The cap is your own moneyThe new loan generally cannot exceed what you actually put in: your documented investment, less any gift funds. Capped at the lower of appraised value at the applicable LTV, or the documented cash purchase price.
It bends timing, not leverageDelayed financing waives the ownership clock. It does not raise the 75% ceiling. Neither do the other exceptions, inherited property, or an LLC-to-individual title transfer.

If you borrowed the purchase money from somewhere else, the proceeds have to repay that first. And discuss this with a lender before you buy in cash rather than after: the paperwork requirements are easier to satisfy when you know about them at closing.

The number that catches people out

If your renovation paperwork will not support the higher appraised value, the lender falls back on cost basis: your original purchase price plus documented, receipted improvement costs.

That is why receipts matter as much as the work. An investor who spent $60,000 on a rehab and kept invoices for $32,000 of it is refinancing against a materially smaller number than the one in their head. Keep contractor invoices, materials receipts and permits from day one, filed against the property rather than in a shoebox.

Rate-and-term or cash-out

Rate-and-term

No equity comes out. You are lowering the carry, moving off an adjustable structure, or changing the term. Underwriting is easier to clear and leverage is less restricted.

Cash-out

You take money off the table: for the next deposit, a renovation, or to clear higher-rate debt. Tighter leverage, a real seasoning clock, and worse pricing than rate-and-term on the same property.

Be honest with yourself about which one you are doing before you apply. Structuring a cash-out as though it were a rate-and-term wastes everyone's time and surfaces at underwriting anyway.

What will not qualify

  • A home you live in, or a second home. This product is for genuine investment property only.
  • FHA financing. Reserved for owner-occupied. Investors need conventional or DSCR.
  • Manufactured homes, log homes and barndominiums. Not eligible collateral on DSCR cash-out programs.
  • A property that will not cover the larger payment. The coverage floor applies to the new loan, not the old one.

Single family, condos and two-to-four unit properties are the widely accepted collateral. A property already financed on DSCR can be refinanced again with another DSCR cash-out, assessed fresh against current rent and current value.

Cash-out refinance questions

How much can I pull out of a rental?

Most programs cap cash-out at around 75% loan-to-value, several points below what the same lender allows on a purchase. Subtract your existing balance and closing costs from that figure to get the cash you would actually receive.

How long do I have to own it first?

Around six months for most DSCR and non-QM cash-out files. Conventional generally wants the existing first mortgage to be at least twelve months old. Delayed financing waives the clock entirely for cash purchases.

What is delayed financing?

An exception for investors who bought with cash and no mortgage. It lets you refinance shortly after closing without waiting out seasoning, capped at the lower of your documented purchase investment or the applicable LTV against the appraisal.

Does pulling cash out hurt my DSCR?

Yes, directly. A larger loan means a larger payment, and the coverage ratio is measured against the new payment. Taking the maximum available cash can push a comfortable property below the coverage floor.

What if my renovation is not documented?

The lender may fall back on cost basis, purchase price plus receipted improvements, rather than the improved appraised value. Keep invoices, receipts and permits filed against the property from the start.

Can I refinance a DSCR loan with another DSCR loan?

Yes. The same leverage ceiling, coverage floor and reserve expectations apply again, evaluated against current rent and current appraised value rather than the original terms.

Is a cash-out priced worse than a rate-and-term?

Generally yes, on the same property, along with tighter leverage and a longer seasoning requirement. If you do not need the equity out, rate-and-term is the cheaper transaction.

Can I do this on a house I live in?

Not through these programs. Owner-occupied primary residences and second homes are excluded, and FHA financing cannot be used for an investment cash-out at all.
